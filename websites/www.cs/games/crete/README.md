# The Island of Crete

Chapter 1, "The Outer Halls", and Chapter 2, "The Winding Ways": twenty tile puzzles in the Labyrinth. Gather every relic, reach the exit once the last relic has opened the gate; the Minotaur takes two steps to every move you make, each along the horizontal gap if that cell is open to it, else along the vertical gap. A catch costs a heart and throws you back to the last safe zone you stood on. The board has two looks (The looks, below): Modern, the default, a 3D scene of grey stone walls on pale sand, and Classic, a Blender-rendered canvas; doors, keys, water, fire, ice, force floors, blocks, buttons, toggle walls and three tools (Medusa's mirror, Daedalus's cow, a honey cake) are the pieces. Medusa's mirror stones the Minotaur on the turn you raise it and for the next three turns, so you get three free moves; while stone it blocks everyone like a wall. Chapter 1 teaches one idea a level; Chapter 2 brings in gravel, which Theseus crosses and the Minotaur and blocks cannot, teaches it in three levels and mixes it with Chapter 1's pieces in seven harder ones. The solver proves each level winnable. Nine trap and trick pieces from Chip's Challenge are in the rules and both solvers too (Pieces, below); of them only gravel is in a released level, and each later chapter brings in the next (the pace is in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-29-crete-chapter-2-design.md`). The campaign is ten chapters; Chapters 1 and 2 are released, and the rest are named in the Lore Book as not yet open.

The page is one frame: a status bar (the level, as "Ch. 1 • Level 3/10" with its name in the tooltip, on a portrait phone on a row of its own; Hearts, Relics, Keys, Gear, Moves, Menu; on a portrait phone Menu moves to the strip's bottom row, between Hints and the look swap), the board, and a control strip (the move pad, one connected cross of four arms round an inert centre square showing Theseus's face (`fx/sprites/theseus-face.png`, cut from his sprite); Wait, Undo, Restart, Hint, Route, Auto, the three tools with their counts and the look swap), each button with a key tab. On a phone Hint, Route and Auto sit behind one Hints button, whose panel opens on a tap and shuts on a choice, a second tap, a tap elsewhere or Q (the focus goes back to Hints, except after a tap elsewhere); Hints shows on while the route is drawn or the play-out runs. Arrows or WASD move, `.` waits, U undoes, R restarts the level, H plays one step of Ariadne's thread, V draws all of it, T plays it out, Z/X/C use the tools, M mutes, N opens the level list, E on the title card opens the level editor, and Q opens the menu and closes whatever is open. Esc is never bound. Any use of the thread marks the try assisted; this browser keeps which levels are cleared and the fewest moves of an unassisted clear. Every campaign level's fewest possible moves (the solver's shortest winning line, kept in `src/campaign/fewest.ts` and checked against the solver by `test/fewest.test.ts`) shows in the level list and on the ending card, which sets a win against it: `best possible 12 (2 over)`, or `perfect` at the fewest. The classic board shows the whole level at the start on every screen, however small its cells (Chapter 1 and 2's 20 × 13 levels take 17 px cells on a 360 × 740 phone). It zooms in, up to 4× or a 64 px cell, whichever is less: two fingers pinch, the wheel zooms toward the pointer, and the + / − / Reset buttons stand at the board's top-right (behind a View button on a narrow touch screen). Zoomed in, the board scrolls inside the frame: a step never changes the zoom, the view pans to keep Theseus two cells inside it, and two fingers or the right or middle button drag it. A double tap or double click, Reset, Restart and a new level show the whole level again; Undo keeps the zoom. A story page is found by taking it and winning that level while holding it. The first time Theseus picks up a kind of item, or touches an active tile (the toggle, brown and red buttons, a teleport, the thief, a snare that holds him, a pop-up wall rising behind him, a bomb, a fake or invisible wall he finds, the open gate he walks through), play waits on a first card with its picture in the board's look, its name, a line of lore and what it does and how to use it; every later pickup or touch slides in a banner (picture, name, a reminder, the tool's key) that fades after 2.5 s and blocks nothing. Nothing else interrupts play: a level opens on its start card alone, a story page opens only its story, and the Minotaur, the moving hazards and the passive tiles have no card. The menu's Dictionary (G) has two tabs: Backpack, what Theseus holds in this try with counts, and Entries, every item and tile, items locked as a silhouette named `???` until first picked up (First meetings and the Dictionary, below). `?seed=` (any value) skips the title card and opens Chapter 1's card.

It is a `play` game: the folder is a complete page (`index.html`) with its own PWA files, and the site wraps it in `play/crete/` without the game knowing.

## Build

`index.html` loads `dist/main.js` with `<script type="module">`. The patterns shared with the other games are in `../_kit/README.md`.

```
cd .. && npm ci                            # once, in games/
node ../_kit/build/build.mjs crete         # type-check src/ and bundle into dist/
node ../_kit/build/build.mjs crete --check # fail if dist/ is missing or stale
php tools/install-files.php                # then rewrite the ?v= versions and sw.js
```

- `dist/` is generated: edit `src/`, rebuild, rerun `install-files.php`, commit all of it. `make test` runs `npm ci` and `--check`, and `deploy.sh` refuses a stale `dist/`.
- `tsconfig.json` extends the kit's base, is strict and lists the DOM-free modules, which compile without page types, so they cannot reach for `document`. `src/tsconfig.dom.json` extends it with the DOM lib for `audio.ts`, `music-decks.ts`, `ui.ts`, the views, `board.ts`, `main.ts` and `solver-client.ts`, and `src/tsconfig.worker.json` with the WebWorker lib for `solver-worker.ts`. Imports carry their `.ts` extension.
- Versions are content hashes written by `tools/install-files.php`, never by hand: `dist/main.js?v=` and `game.css?v=` in `index.html`. The solver worker's `?v=` is its own bundle's hash, which the build puts into `dist/main.js` (`SOLVER_WORKER_V` in `build.json`'s `versions`). `sw.js` hashes the same files plus the rest of `dist/` and `fx/sprites/`, so a changed bundle or atlas makes it stale too.

## The seam

The rules never name the page. `game.ts` takes everything it needs as `GameDeps` (`sfx`, `say`, `progress`, `pages`, `levels`, `timers`, `clock`, `reducedMotion`, `changed`, and `isFirst`, which says which meetings are firsts), so `test/game.test.ts` plays whole levels against recording hooks and a manual clock. `ui.ts` and `audio.ts` are the other ends of those hooks.

A turn that animates draws the state *before* it while the player's figure walks, less each thing the figure has reached (`frameOf` in `game.ts`; the timeline's `taken` gives the moment it lands on each), then the state once the player's action alone has played (`act` in `puzzle/step.ts`, kept as `anim.mid`) while the hazards move, and the state after the turn once the hazards' moves land (the timeline's `hazardEnd`), while the Minotaur steps. Hazards come from the timeline's figures, not the state: the hazards before the turn, moving all at once after the player and before the Minotaur, and the hazards after it once the turn lands, so a hazard destroyed or sent home plays its move and is gone or home only then, and a cloned one appears then, at `hazardEnd` like a cloned block, before the Minotaur steps. Through a teleport a figure jumps to the exit and steps on. The animation pauses the moment the figure reaches a story page, or first meets an item or an active tile (`game.animMs()` stops there and sets `anim.paused` to the stop, `kind` `page` or `meet`), and holds that frame while the page shows its card; `game.resume()` goes on from the same moment, so a slide over several pages stops at each in turn. A play-out waits while its turn has a page still to reach. A key during a slide finishes it and acts at once, exactly once. Reduced motion is read live from `prefers-reduced-motion` and snaps the figures. The thread solves with a cap of 200,000 states; when none is found in that, or the try cannot be won, the toast says the thread goes slack and to undo or restart.

## Pieces

The trap and trick pieces, by their character in the level text. The code uses the plain name; the game and the help text use the myth name where there is one.

| Char | Plain name | In the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `&` | `trap` | Daedalus's snare |
| `-` | `button-brown` | Brown button |
| `+` | `button-red` | Red button |
| `;` | `popup` | Pop-up wall |
| `:` | `gravel` | Gravel |
| `W` | `fake-wall` | Fake wall |
| `I` | `invisible-wall` | Invisible wall |
| `@` | `teleport` | Teleport |
| `$` | `thief` | Thief |
| `!` | `bomb` | Bomb |
| `C` | `clone` | Clone machine |
| `o` | `ball` | Bronze ball |
| `w` | `glider` | Harpy |
| `e` | `fireball` | Talos's fire |

A hazard (`o`, `w`, `e`) stands on floor; a clone machine's cell is terrain `clone` with nothing on it. Two headers go with them, each a `LevelError` at the header when its count or a word is wrong:

- `facing:` one direction (`up`, `down`, `left`, `right`) per hazard on the grid, in reading order; required exactly when the grid has a hazard.
- `machines:` one `<template>-<dir>` per `C`, in reading order, the template `block`, `ball`, `glider` or `fireball`; required exactly when the grid has a `C`.

Links are never written. As in Chip's Challenge, each brown button controls the first snare after it in reading order, wrapping to the first, and each red button the first clone machine after it; a button with nothing to control does nothing. `parse` derives `Level.links`.

- **Daedalus's snare**: whoever stands on it (Theseus, a block, a hazard, the Minotaur) is held while no linked brown button is held down by Theseus, a block or a hazard (never the Minotaur or the cow). Theseus's move while held is a bump; waiting and tools are turns. A block in a held snare cannot be pushed. The snare keeps no state of its own: it holds or not by what stands on its buttons now.
- **Pop-up wall**: floor until Theseus leaves it by any means (a step, a slide, a throw, a teleport), then wall. Nothing else enters it.
- **Gravel**: Theseus crosses it; blocks, the Minotaur and hazards do not.
- **Fake wall**: wall to everyone until Theseus bumps it, which opens it and costs a turn.
- **Invisible wall**: looks like floor and is wall; Theseus's bump shows it and costs a turn. Slides, pushed blocks and teleport exits reveal neither kind.
- **Teleport**: Theseus or a block comes out of the next teleport in reading order (wrapping), onto the cell beyond it in the same direction, and arrives there in full; a blocked exit is skipped for the next. Wall to the Minotaur and hazards; a lone teleport is wall.
- **Thief**: takes all of Theseus's gear; keys and tools stay.
- **Bomb**: a block or a hazard arriving on it is destroyed with it; Theseus arriving on it loses a heart and is thrown back as in a catch, and the bomb is gone. Wall to the Minotaur.
- **Clone machine**: wall to everyone. Each time something arrives on its red button it makes its template in front of it, facing its way, when that cell is free.
- **Bronze ball**: goes straight; turns back when blocked. **Harpy**: turns left, then right, then back; crosses water. **Talos's fire**: turns right, then left, then back; crosses fire. A hazard stuck every way stays. A hazard keeps to the ground the Minotaur keeps to, and a bomb, which destroys it; ice and force floors carry it as they carry him. It never enters a cell with an object, a block, the cow, the Minotaur or another hazard. Theseus stepping into a hazard, or a hazard into him, is a catch. The Minotaur and a hazard never share a cell, stone or not.

A turn goes in a fixed order and stops at its first catch: Theseus's action, then each hazard once, in reading order of the cells they stood on when the phase began (a hazard that moves keeps its place in that order), then the Minotaur's two steps.

The rulings the pieces need:

- **Sent home**: when a catch throws Theseus onto a hazard, or the Minotaur home onto one, that hazard goes back to its start (a level hazard's own cell; a clone's, the cell in front of its machine). If that is taken, it goes to the nearest cell it may stand on, breadth-first from its start over every cell, ties in reading order, never a bomb; only when no cell on the board can take it is it removed. Being set down is no arrival: it presses no button, though a snare it is set down in holds it.
- **The cow**: a hazard never enters the cow's cell, but one that moves onto Theseus standing on his cow catches him and the cow is lost. The cow never holds a brown button down.
- **Hazards and the toggle button**: a hazard on `_` flips the toggle walls, as Theseus and blocks do.
- **Teleport chains**: there are none. A teleport beyond an exit makes that exit blocked. Out of a teleport, Theseus pushes a block only when he entered it by his own step, not on a ride; with every exit blocked he stays on the teleport he entered, and a pushed block's push is refused.
- **Bomb catches**: a bomb under Theseus is a catch (`caught` with `by: 'bomb'`), so a search that refuses catches refuses it and the turn stops there. A hazard's catch is `by: 'hazard'`, the Minotaur's `by: 'minotaur'`.
- **Clones arrive in full**: a cloned block settles like a pushed one (it sinks, explodes, presses, slides); a cloned hazard presses buttons and meets a bomb on its cell, and first moves in the next hazard phase. One made during the hazard phase joins the end of the list and does not move until the next phase. A red button pressed by a clone's arrival fires again; each firing needs a free cell, so a chain ends.
- **No clone on the cell Theseus enters**: a clone is never made on Theseus, nor on the cell he is stepping into while his own push presses the button.

## The solver

`solve` in `src/puzzle/solver.ts` is exact: it returns a shortest winning line, proves a level unwinnable, or says it gave up at its cap (2,000,000 stored situations; capped is never "no win"). It is A\* (`astar.ts`) over situations packed into a few 32-bit words (`pack.ts`). A situation holds everything that can change during a try and nothing else: the cells of Theseus, the Minotaur, the cow and the throw-back, the Minotaur's stone turns, hearts, relics still needed, story pages, keys, tools and gear, one bit for each cell whose terrain changes once (a door or fake wall opens, water fills, a pop-up rises, an invisible wall shows, a bomb goes off) and one for the toggle walls, the objects and blocks where they lie, and each hazard's cell, facing and home (the home decides its kind). Where no machine clones a hazard the hazards pack as one slot per level hazard; otherwise per cell a hazard could ever stand on. A snare holds no state: whether it holds is read from its buttons. The search runs on that, with an estimate that never overstates the moves left (`heuristic.ts`) and pruning that drops a situation another one already seen beats on every resource that only helps (`prune.ts`). The nodes live in typed arrays (`store.ts`); a spilling store keeps the first ones in memory and the rest in fixed-size records on pages of a file (`paged.ts`), and a file that fails ends the search capped with the failure as its reason. `searching` runs the same search in batches, yielding its progress between them; `measure` has `measuring` the same way, its catch search (`catching`, a breadth-first search with no estimate, so its progress reports a best f and start estimate of 0) included. The game's thread help solves in the page, as it always answers within a few hundred milliseconds on Chapter 1's levels.

The editor and any long search run in a Web Worker instead. `startSearch(levelText, kind)` in `src/solver-client.ts` (kind `solve` or `measure`) returns a job: `progress(cb)` (expansions, situations stored, the best f, the start's estimate, and `storage`), `stop()`, `resume()`, `cancel()` and `result`. One worker serves every search; a new search cancels the one before it, stopped or not, whose result rejects with the host's message. `cancel()` ends a job for good: `result` rejects with `CancelledError` at once, no more progress arrives, and the host closes the job's node file like a replaced search's. A worker that errors (it failed to load, or threw) is terminated and its searches reject, and the next search starts a fresh one; where no worker can be made, the search rejects at once. In the worker (`src/solver-host.ts`):

- A finished result (solved, or proved unwinnable; for a measure, nothing unknown) is cached under the kind, the solver's version and the SHA-256 of the exact level text, in the origin private file system (`crete-results/`), and a second search of the same text answers from it with no progress. The version is a hash of the worker's own bundle, which holds every rule and the solver, so a new build never serves an old build's answer; when the bundle cannot be read, nothing is cached. After each put the cache is trimmed (`evictions` in `src/puzzle/cache.ts`): entries of another version go first, then all but the newest `RESULT_LIMIT = 256` by file `lastModified`. A cached line is served only if it still wins the level on its last move, and a cached measure with no `line` is searched again. Any storage error reads as a miss; nothing throws to the page.
- The search runs 4,096 expansions per batch, posts its progress, then lets messages in: stop waits there until resume, which goes on with the same search.
- Its nodes past the first 65,536 go to a node file in the origin private file system (`crete-nodes/`), written through a sync access handle in 64 KiB pages of which 64 (4 MiB) are cached. The file's size is the handle's, and a short read or write throws, so the search ends capped with a reason. When the file system is missing or refuses (a private window, no quota), the nodes stay in memory and progress says `storage: 'memory'`. The file is closed and deleted when the search ends or is cancelled; one a closed tab left behind is deleted by the first search six hours after it was made.
- Memory is bounded before the cap: per stored node, the A\* arrays and heap take 19 bytes, pruning's index at most 8 × words + 20, and the store 12 bytes with the file (its hash and table) or 4 × words + 21 without; every array grows by doubling, so to at most twice that. With the file the 65,536 nodes in memory (4 × words + 9 bytes each) and the page cache come on top. The worker's cap is the solver's 2,000,000 or less, so that the total stays within 384 MiB: Chapter 1's levels (3 words) and anything up to 5 words keep 2,000,000 with the file; 10 words get 1,508,578, and a level that packs into 23 words 833,701. Should memory run out below that anyway, a solve ends capped with the failure as its reason and a measure rejects with it. The disk holds only the nodes themselves: pruning's index, the A\* arrays, the heap and the store's hash stay in memory, so the file extends a search's reach by a constant factor, not without bound.

`test/solver-big.test.ts` proves a generated 40 × 40 level (keys, doors, two blocks, a Minotaur; 145 moves) in about a second and checks it against the reference.

## What makes a good level

Winnable is not enough: a level the solver wins in a few hundred situations, with the Minotaur walled off, is no fight. `measure` in `src/puzzle/difficulty.ts` runs the solver three ways and reports the shortest line (`line`, as move letters; `''` when unsolved, so Check holds the proof a share link carries) and the situations stored to find it (`explored`: the A\* search with pruning in `src/puzzle/solver.ts`), the shortest line with the Minotaur taken off the board (`minotaurDelta` is how many moves he adds), whether that Minotaur-free line still wins when played on the real level (`greedyWins`), the shortest line that is never caught, and the catches on the shortest line. A search that hits its cap reports unknown, never none. `hardPieces(level)` names the pieces that make a level hard ("4 blocks", "2 keys and 2 doors"), the most numerous first.

It also labels the level and plays the plain walk:

- **chase** or **solo** (`label`): `canCatch` searches breadth-first from the start and stops at the first turn the Minotaur catches Theseus. A catch by him makes the level chase; a hazard's or a bomb's never does. A level with no Minotaur, or one whose search ends with no catch by him (he taunts Theseus from behind walls or water), is solo; when the cells the Minotaur could ever reach and the cells Theseus could ever reach do not meet, that is proved without a search (a door counts open only when its key is on the board, water only when a block or a block machine could fill it; a fake wall or a bomb counts open to the Minotaur; Theseus goes through every piece but a wall, and from any teleport to every cell beside any teleport). A capped search labels it unknown. The search yields its progress in batches like the others, so the worker can report and stop it.
- **The plain walk** (`naiveWins`): the shortest path from Theseus to the exit over plain floor, ignoring every piece (only walls stop it) and taking the relics the gate needs on the way, played as moves on the real level. `naiveWins` is true when that walk wins, false when it fails or there is no plain path.

`challengeProblems` checks a measure against `BAR`, the campaign's bar, and lists what falls short:

- Every level with a Minotaur: he shapes the route. He adds moves, or the line that ignores him loses.
- A chapter's challenge levels (those after its teaching stretch, `teaching` in `src/campaign/chapters.ts`; Chapter 1 teaches in levels 1 to 8, Chapter 2 in levels 1 to 3) are a real fight: the line that ignores the Minotaur loses, he adds at least 8 moves, the search stores at least 20 times the situations of the chapter's hardest teaching level, and the line is no shorter than its longest teaching level's. `rampProblems` keeps each challenge level from storing fewer situations than the one before it.

`sharedProblems` checks a measure against `SHARED_BAR`, the bar every shared level must clear. It never needs a Minotaur, so a solo level clears it on puzzle difficulty alone:

- The level is proved winnable, and the plain walk does not win.
- The quickest win takes at least 40 moves, and the solver stores at least 12,000 situations to find it.
- A chase level whose Minotaur adds at least 8 moves (`BAR.minotaurDelta`), and whose line that ignores him loses, needs only 30 moves and 6,000 situations.

## The looks

The Look setting (the new-game card after New Game, and the menu's Board section, both as two pictures from `fx/looks/`) picks the board. It is stored as `crete-look` (`modern` or `classic`, default `modern`); View is stored as `crete-view` (`angled` or `top`, default `top`) and shows in the menu in Modern only; Walls is stored as `crete-walls` (`solid`, `ghosted` or `low`, default `solid`) and shows in the menu in Modern while the view is Angled.

- **Classic** is the board of `src/board.ts` on the `#stage` canvas: the Blender sprites, drawn every frame while it shows.
- **Modern** is `#stage3d`, a three.js scene (`src/modern/`): flagstone floor, walls, a model for every piece and figure, torch and lamp light, then a crisp finish: a tone map, a light warm grade and a vignette, anti-aliased (four samples a pixel for the scene, then FXAA); no pass blurs or repaints the picture. The figures wear a thin dark rim that parts them from the floor, as Classic's sprites are outlined. Theseus stands out most: brighter, faintly glowing colours, a little larger than the others, on a green ring with a dark edge in his marker's colour. Walls and wall tops are cool grey stone with a dark outline on each wall top's open edge, floors pale warm sand in wide flagstones over light mortar, with a soft shade at each wall's foot, and a near-black rim round the board. Models are built in code from primitives and procedural textures; the sprite atlas only supplies the mini map. The board is drawn only when something changes (a move, the camera easing, a wall fading, a figure turning) and the tile loops (water ripples, fire, flames, discs, torches, the floating markers' bob and the floating block's bob) tick at 12 fps, only while one is on screen, the tab is visible and motion is not reduced. At rest it draws nothing. It draws at the device's pixel ratio (at most 2). A render quality ladder measures the frame rate of animating frames only (idle waits, stalls and the first frames after a level opens or the tab returns are left out): under 40 fps over two seconds of animation it drops shadows, then FXAA, then the pixel ratio to 1, one step at a time; over 55 fps for three seconds it steps back up, unless that step has proved too slow twice.
- **Walls**: Solid, Ghosted (a wall that hides Theseus, or any piece or figure, fades to a third) and Low (walls a third as high). Theseus is never hidden behind a wall in any mode. Walls never hide the markers.
- **Items**: every object Theseus can take and every hazard is sized to fill about 0.8 of its cell, as Classic's sprites do, so each can be named at the whole-level view on a phone; it wears the figures' thin dark rim, has bright, faintly glowing colours and stands on a dark disc that parts it from the pale floor. An object stands on a plate: the disc with a thin cream ring at its edge. A relic, a key, the mirror, boots, the scroll and the cow lean back toward the camera so their faces show from above; a key's bow and gem are in its door's colour. Measured on the zoomed-out phone view of the Tiles level, every object and hazard is at least 3:1 against the floor round it.
- **Floating markers**: a small glass crystal, point down, with a thin dark rim, floats over Theseus (green) and the Minotaur (red, stone or not). It hangs just above his head as the screen shows it, so it sits over him in both views (in Top-Down its point stays inside his cell), over the walls, bobbing gently (still when motion is reduced), never under 14 CSS px across however far out the view is; while its figure is on screen a marker slides inside the board's edge rather than being cut off. Markers are decoration: they take no taps and say nothing to a screen reader.
- **Switchable choices**: `src/modern/variants.ts` holds the look's switches and what ships: items on plates (or a cyan or white crystal over each object and a violet one over each hazard), and, all off, a beacon over Theseus with his outline through walls, cut-away walls in Angled, a steeper Angled, pale wall tops with dark floor joints, a Peek button, a Map button and an inset view of the whole level. A browser can override them for sample renders with the `crete-variant` key (JSON).
- **Blocks in water**: a block pushed into water fills it (the rules make the cell dirt); the modern board draws the cell as the block floating in the water, its top just above the surface, bobbing slowly. Going in, it splashes, dips under and rises to float; under reduced motion it simply floats. Undo or a restart empties the water again.
- **Camera**: the pitch is the View setting's: 45° and turned 15° (Angled), or 75° and square to the board like the classic look (Top-Down), so the whole maze shows and a wall hides only about a quarter of the cell behind it; switching eases between them. One finger swipes Theseus a cell the way it went on the screen (`src/swipe.ts`: at least 24 px within 700 ms; the classic board takes the same swipe) and never moves the camera; the left button drags to turn; two fingers or the right or middle button pan; twisting two fingers turns (Angled only: Top-Down does not turn, and hides the turn buttons); pinching zooms about the fingers and the wheel zooms toward the pointer, from about five rows in to the whole level turned any way out, however big it is; a double tap or double click resets the turn (to the view's own) and the zoom; the camera buttons at the board's top-right turn 15° either way, zoom in and out, and reset (a View button shows and hides them on a narrow touch screen). Every level opens on the whole level, centred, with about a cell's margin round it, as Classic shows it, and Reset view returns there; while the view is there, Theseus's steps leave the camera still. There are no camera keys. Every camera control is ignored while a card is open. Once the player zooms, the camera follows Theseus and keeps the level centred where it fits the view; a pan, a mini-map tap or a wheel or pinch zoom away from the centre looks elsewhere until Theseus's next step brings the camera back to him.
- **Moves follow the screen**: the arrows, WASD, the pad move along the nearest screen direction of the turned view (`modern/screen-move.ts`); Classic and a view at its default turn move as the grid does.
- **Mini Map**: off unless the menu's Mini Map switch (Modern only; this browser keeps it as `crete-minimap`) is on: at the board's bottom-left, the whole level in Classic's sprites with the camera's view outlined; a tap or drag on it sends the camera there. Classic never shows it.
- **WebGL fallback**: with no WebGL, or if `dist/modern.js` fails to load, the game shows Classic and the toast says so once; the stored choice stays `modern`. If the browser loses the WebGL context, Classic shows until the browser gives it back, then Modern returns.
- **Specs**: the e2e specs that read Classic's pixels (`crete-play`, `crete-layout`, `crete-pieces`) pin `crete-look` to `classic`; `crete-modern.spec.ts` covers Modern.

## Layout

The frame (`#frame`, a size container) fills the window and stacks the status bar (`#status-root`), the board (`#board`, holding the classic view `#stage-view` with its `#stage` canvas, the classic zoom buttons `#classic-cam` (they wear the Modern camera buttons' look), the Modern area `#modern` with its `#stage3d` canvas and overlay (one of the two shows, by the Look), and a toast over its foot that shows the newest log line and fades) and the control strip (`#strip-root`). `ui.ts` fits the canvas with `wholeCell` (`fit.ts`) over the view's space and `boardRows` (the top row's rise included), then reads the size the canvas was actually given: `board.ts` snaps a cell to whole device pixels, which can round it up, so the cell shrinks until the canvas fits. That whole-level cell is zoom 1; zoomed in, the canvas is redrawn at the zoomed cell, snapped to whole device pixels (never a scaled bitmap), and `#stage-view` scrolls (`follow.ts` keeps the player two cells inside the view; the toast and the zoom buttons stay on the visible board). The pointer gestures are the Modern board's reader (`modern/gestures.ts`), without the turn. The cards are inside the frame, so full screen keeps them. A key tab is the kit's `.kit-key-tab`; it shows only with a mouse and a frame at least 760 px wide. A phone is a frame at most 760 px wide in portrait or at most 500 px tall in landscape (`phone()` in `ui.ts`, matching the container queries in `game.css`). A portrait phone stacks the status bar in two rows and puts the strip under the board: the pad down one edge and, at least 14 px away, three rows (Wait, Undo and Restart; the tools; Hints, then Menu and the look swap, in that order on either side); a landscape phone puts the pad and the actions in the margins either side of the board. The pad stands at the strip's left edge, or its right one when the menu's Arrows says Right (the frame then wears `pad-right` and every layout is mirrored).

## Cards, Q and settings

The cards are a stack (`layers.ts`): title, the new-game card, menu, story, chapter, Lore, the Dictionary, a first card, levels, a level's start card, ending, the level editor, and a shared level's card. Only the top one shows. Q (the kit's `isBackKey`) closes the top card; with nothing open it opens the menu (on the title card too, over it). New Game (N) on the title card opens the new-game card, "How should the Labyrinth look?": Modern and Classic as pictures above their names with a line under each, the one drawn now picked; a pick (a click, or left and right) takes effect at once and is kept, Enter the Halls (Enter) goes on and Back (Q) returns to the title card. Once a level is cleared, and until every released level is, the title card also offers Continue (Enter, and focused; its tooltip and accessible name say where it goes: "Continue at Chapter 2, Level 3"): the first released level not cleared, in campaign order. It opens that level's chapter card, whose Enter starts it. Enter the Halls closes it and opens Chapter 1's card (under "Chapter 1 of 10": its opening words and quotation, Enter the Halls and Levels), whatever this browser has cleared; Enter starts Chapter 1, Level 1. Only a level picked from the list, or a link, starts anywhere else. N anywhere opens the levels list over what is showing: a tab for each of the ten chapters (the unreleased ones disabled; it opens on the chapter of the level in play) and a button per level of the shown chapter with its title and whether it is cleared, cleared with help or not yet; up and down (or j and k) move between the levels and Enter opens the one chosen. Q returns to what was under it. A level from outside the campaign (a try from the editor, or a shared level) records nothing, has no chapter and opens no Lore entry for its story pages: its start and ending cards have their own kicker ("Your level" or "Shared level"), the ending card has no Next Level or Levels, a try's has Back to the Editor, and a shared level's adds a line setting the win against the author's best. A campaign level's end opens the ending card once its last step has landed: after a win Next Level (Enter; a chapter's first level shows that chapter's opening card first), Retry (R) and Levels (N), with the titles of any story pages the win took; after a loss Undo and Restart. Next Level always opens the next level in campaign order, whatever is cleared. Clearing a chapter's level 10 opens its closing card over the ending card: under "End of Chapter 1", its words, the win's moves and best possible ("Level 10 cleared: 76 moves · best possible 76 · perfect"), and On to Chapter 2, which opens that chapter's opening card, whose Enter starts its Level 1; when the next chapter is not released, a line says so and the button is Continue, back to the ending card; Q always goes back to the ending card. The menu's kicker names the level in play as the status bar does ("Ch. 1 • Level 3/10"). The menu holds three sections under small headings: Sound and Screen (switches for Sound, Music, Music in the Background and Full Screen), Board (the two looks as pictures; in Modern, View and Walls; and Arrows: Left or Right, the edge of the strip the move pad stands at) and The Story (the Lore Book, the Dictionary, the Story Log and How to Play, folded); then Back to the Editor (during a try from the editor) or Shared Level (its card, during a shared level whose link is the open one), and Quit This Run on its own. On a frame at least 760 px wide the sections stand in two columns. The Lore Book lists each released chapter's opening card, its story pages (a found page opens in full; the rest say which level hides them), its closing card once seen, and one line for each unreleased chapter; Q goes from an open entry back to the list, then closes the book. Every card works from the keyboard: up and down (or W and S, J and K) move the focus between its enabled buttons, clamped at the ends, and Enter or Space presses the focused one (where Enter already means something, as on the chapter, start and ending cards, it keeps that meaning while the focus is on the card's default button or on nothing); left and right switch the chapter tabs of the level list. A share link in the address (a fragment `#L<digits>…`) opens its card (the level's name, its rating, `rating…` until the page's own search is in or a line saying it waits for the editor's Check, and the author's best to beat, with Play and Edit a Copy, which opens the editor on a copy as one undo step), Q on it goes to the title card, or back to the menu when the menu opened it during its level, and the browser's Back to no fragment goes to the title card; a link that cannot be opened leaves the title card up, saying why. Taking a story page opens its Lore entry over the board the moment Theseus reaches it, with the turn's animation paused there (the thread's play-out stops too); its button is Go On (Enter), and Enter, Space or Q close it and the turn goes on, pausing again at the next page a slide takes. ## Music

The title theme, "Crete: title theme" (`music/compose/pieces/crete_title.py`, draft 3), plays on every screen: the title card, the story, a chapter's card, the Lore Book, a level and its cards, the editor and a shared level's card, until the game has music for play. It starts once the music may sound (Sound on, Music on, and the browser has had a gesture) and then runs on, looping, whatever opens or closes: no card or level starts, stops or crossfades it. Only the Music switch, Sound (M), the browser's autoplay rule and, with Music in the Background off, a hidden page stop or start it.

The theme streams from media elements rather than being decoded (four minutes decoded would hold about 90 MB on a phone), each element through its own gain onto the kit's music bus (`src/music-decks.ts`). The render's reverb tail is folded onto its start, so the file runs on from its end into its start; the browser's own media loop leaves a gap there, so there are two elements, both made and loading when the music first starts. As the first reaches its end the second starts the file from the beginning, so every pass is the whole file and the beat never slips; the two take turns. The file has nothing past its end to overlap, so the join is a 30 ms equal-power dip (the first fades out over its last 30 ms, the second fades in over its first) that only hides a decoder's gap. The loop point sits in a quiet stretch of drone. The idle element is played once silently and paused when the music starts, and again on each tap or key press until that takes, so the browser lets the loop point start it from a timer; should it still refuse, the playing element starts the file again itself and the next loop point tries the other again. A browser that blocks autoplay refuses the first play; the first tap or key press (the gesture that wakes the speakers) starts it. A play cut short by a pause (AbortError), or refused after a newer play of the same element, is not taken as a block. Timers in a hidden page run late, so the loop point is also taken from the playing element's `ended` event: a hidden page's loop may gain a gap as long as the timer was late, but never stops. By default a hidden page keeps the music going; the menu's Music in the Background switch (On by default, kept under `crete-music-hidden`) set to Off makes a hidden page hold the music where it is, and showing the page goes on from there.

The files are Opus in WebM (`audio/music/title.webm`) and, for browsers without it (Safari), AAC (`title.m4a`), both 96 kb/s, about 2.7 MB each; `main.ts` asks `canPlayType` which to stream, with the file's content hash (`build.json` versions) on its address. The service worker does not precache them and lets the elements' range requests go to the network, so offline play goes on without music. The music bus plays at 0.5 under the effects. The Music switch in the menu mutes the music bus alone and stops the elements; Sound (M) silences the effects and the music.

To remake it, in `websites/www.cs`: `make music-render PIECE=crete-title VERSION=3`, then `make music-render PIECE=crete-title VERSION=3 CUT=full`, and copy `music/pieces/crete-title/v3/cuts/full.*` here as `audio/music/title.*`; rebuild so the hash changes.

## The editor

The editor is a view on the game page, not a page of its own. `games/crete/#edit` opens it, and so does Make a Level on the title card (E). It is a layer over the game frame: a board to paint on, a palette of brushes (terrain, objects, hazards, machines, the player and the Minotaur, erase; O turns a brush's direction), a bar with Undo, Redo, Try It and Leave, boxes for the board's rows and columns (3 to 64), the hearts and the relics needed, and a Check panel and a Share panel in the side column. On a narrow screen the side column is a drawer that the Pieces button opens, and Check's progress and result show in a strip outside it. While the editor is the top layer every key goes to it (see Keys); Q closes a dialog, else leaves the editor for the title card and takes `#edit` off the address.

- **The draft** and its undo history live in memory for the page's life: a reload loses them. Opening a draft (Edit a Copy, `__creteTest.editor.load`) is one undo step on the history the page already has, and the first draft of a page has none beneath it.
- **Try It** plays the draft on the full game screen (start card, status bar, strip, menu, Ariadne's thread, ending card) as a level from outside the campaign. It records no progress and finds no pages. The menu and the ending card offer Back to the Editor, which settles the game and shows the draft as it was. A try won with no help is the author's best for that level text, which a link then carries. Where Check has proved this exact draft, the thread follows its line instead of solving in the page.
- **Check** runs when asked, never while editing: one `measure` in the solver's worker (`src/editor/check.ts`), with a progress bar, Stop and Keep Searching. It proves the level solvable, keeps the shortest winning line and rates how hard the level is. A change to the draft makes the result out of date. The worker runs one search at a time, so a rating wanted for an open link waits for a Check, and a Check started during a rating comes first.
- **Share** needs three letters of initials (the kit's `validInitials`: A to Z, never a word on the blocked list; kept in this browser under `crete-initials`). It checks the draft if it changed since the last Check, and offers a link only for a level that is solved, with a line that replays to a win, and that `sharedProblems` (`src/puzzle/difficulty.ts`) finds hard enough; otherwise it says what is missing. The link is shown only once it opens again in this page. Copy copies it.
- **The link** is `<game page>#L1.<payload>`. The payload is base64url, without padding, of a `deflate-raw` stream of UTF-8 text: `by: ABC`, `best: N` when the author has a best, `line: <move letters>` (the shortest winning line, `lineText` in `src/puzzle/cache.ts`), a blank line, then the level text with an empty title and intro and no `pages:`. Nothing is sent to the server. `L1` is the format version; another version reads as made by a newer version of the game. Limits, checked in this order: the version; at most `ENCODED_MAX` (16,384) characters after `#L1.`, before anything is decompressed; base64url; at most `DECODED_MAX` (32,768) characters decompressed, enforced chunk by chunk; the header, whose initials must pass `validInitials` unchanged; a board of 3 to 64 on each side, checked on the raw grid before `parse` runs; `parse`; no title, intro or page in the level text (any is damaged); a line of at most `MAX_LINE` (18,000) moves; a line of known move letters; a `best` that is an integer no smaller than the line; and last, a line that replays to a win on its last move. A link that fails any of them shows a message on the title card and never a board. A level too large to fit is refused at Share with `This level is too large to share as a link.`
- **The generated name.** A link carries no free text: no title, intro or page ids. A shared level is named `Level XXXX by ABC`, where `XXXX` is `levelCode(text)` (`src/share/name.ts`): the first 20 bits of the SHA-256 of the link's level text, five bits at a time, high bits first, as characters of `ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789`.
- **Opening a link.** Only a fragment `#L<digits>…` is a link; `#edit` opens the editor and any other fragment is ignored. The card shows the name, the rating (the page's own `measure` search runs on opening, off the main thread) and the best to beat. Play opens the level from its start (Ariadne's thread follows the link's line without solving); Edit a Copy opens the editor on the level with no initials or best, as one undo step.
- **The owner flag.** The kit pref `crete-owner` set to `on` shows the campaign's own tools: an id, a title and an intro for the level, the page brush with a page id per page cell, and Copy for trials.ts, which copies ``{ id: '<id>', text: `<level text>` },`` with the text escaped for a TypeScript template string (backslashes, backticks and `${`) and the id escaped for a quoted one (quotes, backslashes and line breaks; `trial-new` when it is empty). It is a convenience for the author, not a security boundary: the level text a link carries has none of these fields.
- **The test hook.** `__creteTest.editor` is `{ open, close, key, bestFor, draft, load(text) }`; `load` opens the editor on a level text. `tests/e2e/crete-editor.spec.ts` builds and tries a level, shares a level and wins it from the link on a phone-sized page, and opens a damaged link, a link in the address, Edit a Copy and the browser's Back.

| I | Open the story card (title card only) |
| F, L, G | Full screen; open the Lore Book; open the Dictionary (menu only) |
| Up, Down, J, K | Move the focus between the top card's buttons; Enter or Space presses the focused one |
| Left, Right | Switch chapter tabs in the level list, or the Dictionary's tabs; pick Modern or Classic on the new-game card |
| Q | Close the top card; with none open, open the menu; on the title card, open the menu |

While the editor is the top layer every key goes to it: U and Ctrl/Cmd+Z undo, Y and Ctrl/Cmd+Y or Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z redo, P tries the draft, C checks it, O turns the brush's direction, and Q closes a dialog or else leaves the editor. Esc is never bound. `src/keys.ts` is the one table: the key tabs, the help text and the bindings all read it; the tools' keys come from `TOOLS` in `src/puzzle/tiles.ts`. A chord (Ctrl, Cmd, Alt) is the browser's, never a game key.

## The art

Every sprite is rendered by Blender from scenes built in code: `fx/blender/render_tiles.py` writes one `.blend` per sprite beside it (Git LFS), renders each through one camera and light rig, and tiles the frames into `fx/sprites/tiles.png` with `tiles.json` naming where each sprite sits (`src/puzzle/tiles.ts` lists the names). It needs Blender 4.2 or newer (`brew install --cask blender`) and the system `python3` with Pillow. To regenerate, from `fx/blender/`:

```
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b -P render_tiles.py -- --out ../sprites
```

`--only a,b` renders a few sprites into `<out>/_only/` for a quick look and leaves the atlas alone; every option is described at the top of the script. The script also writes `preview-16.png` (each sprite at 16 px) and `board-preview.png` beside itself. After a new atlas, rebuild and rerun `install-files.php` so `sw.js` hashes it. `fx/CREDITS.md` states that no outside images, models or fonts are used.

The title card's picture is the Labyrinth's entrance at night: cyclopean ashlar, red Minoan columns with black capitals, horns of consecration on the lintel, a gold labrys over the door, two bronze braziers, and inside the door a stair going down to the fire of the depths, where the Minotaur stands as a silhouette in shafts of light through a haze; the upper part is night sky, where the card stands. `fx/blender/title_card.py` builds the scene in code and renders it crisp (EEVEE, the AgX tone map and a light grade; no paint or blur pass) in two framings: `fx/title/title-wide.webp` (1920 × 1080) behind the title card on a landscape frame and `fx/title/title-tall.webp` (1080 × 1920) on a portrait one (`game.css`, a container query on the frame), each under 250 KB, with a scrim that darkens the sky behind the card. To regenerate both, and the scene as `fx/blender/title.blend` (Git LFS), run `tools/render-title.sh` from this folder (Blender 4.2 or newer on the PATH, or `BLENDER=`; ImageMagick 7), then rerun `install-files.php` so `sw.js` hashes them. The share picture and the hub tile show play, not the title card, so they do not change with it.

`tiles-test.html` (entry `src/tiles-page.ts`, bundled to `dist/tiles-test.js`) is a test page, not deployed: it plays a level that holds every piece on the board canvas, and draws each sprite alone in a strip at a chosen size. Open it from the local site at `games/crete/tiles-test.html`.

## Test hook

`window.__creteTest` is `{ game, TRIALS, solve, initial, trialLevel, LEVEL_IDS, CHAPTERS, solveInWorker, playLevel, parse, editor, modern }`: the real controller (`game.run.now` is the current state with `moves`, `hearts` and `outcome`), the ten levels, the exact solver, a state from a level, level `i`'s level, the released levels' ids in play order, the chapter table, and `solveInWorker(levelText)`, which solves in the worker and resolves with `{ result, progress }` (every progress report). `playLevel(level, { outside?, line? })` opens a level from outside the campaign (a try unless `outside` says otherwise), with `line` as its proven line; `parse` reads level text; `editor` is the Editor plus `load(text)`, which opens the editor on that level. `modern` is `{ frames(), yaw(), active }`: the Modern board's drawn frames, its camera yaw and whether it is showing. The browser specs read it to take the solver's winning line and press it through the page's own buttons and keys.

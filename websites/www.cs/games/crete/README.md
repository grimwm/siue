# The Island of Crete

Chapters 1 to 10, "The Outer Halls", "The Winding Ways", "The Closing Ways", "The False Walls", "The Snares of Daedalus", "The Folding Halls", "The Bronze Guard", "The Harpies' Den", "Daedalus's Workshop" and "The Heart of the Labyrinth": a hundred tile puzzles in the Labyrinth. Gather every relic, reach the exit once the last relic has opened the Daedalus Gate (a pit across the way that bronze bars slide over, a grid Theseus crosses and the Minotaur cannot); the Minotaur takes two steps to every move you make, each along the horizontal gap if that cell is open to it, else along the vertical gap. A catch costs a heart and throws you back to the last safe zone you stood on. The board has two looks (The looks, below): Modern, the default, a 3D scene of grey stone walls on pale sand, and Classic, a Blender-rendered canvas; doors, keys, water, fire, ice, force floors, blocks, buttons, toggle walls and three tools (Medusa's mirror, Daedalus's cow, a honey cake) are the pieces. Medusa's mirror stones the Minotaur on the turn you raise it and for the next three turns, so you get three free moves; while stone it blocks everyone like a wall. Each honey cake adds a heart, with no limit but the cakes a level holds (3 to 4 to 5); hearts never carry from one level to the next, and every try (Restart included) starts at the level's own hearts, three in every campaign level. The packed state's hearts field is sized for the most a try can reach, the starting hearts plus one a cake (`src/puzzle/pack.ts`; `maxHearts` in `src/puzzle/state.ts`). Chapter 1 teaches one idea a level; each later chapter brings in one new piece, teaches it in three levels and mixes it with the earlier pieces in seven harder ones: gravel, which Theseus crosses and the Minotaur and blocks cannot (Chapter 2); the pop-up wall, which rises behind whoever leaves it (Chapter 3); the fake and invisible walls (Chapter 4); Daedalus's snare with its brown button, which holds whoever steps in, the beast too, until something weighs the button down (Chapter 5); the teleport, which sends whoever steps in, or a block pushed in, out of the next one along, and is wall to the beast (Chapter 6); the bronze ball, which rolls straight on its own every turn, turns back at whatever stops it, costs a heart at a touch, blocks the beast as he blocks it, and presses the buttons it rolls over (Chapter 7); the Harpy and Talos's fire, which move like the ball but turn left and right where it turns back, the Harpy flying over water and the fire running through fire (Chapter 8); and the thief, which takes all of Theseus's gear, the bomb, which costs a heart and is gone, bursting with any block or hazard that arrives on it, and the clone machine, which turns out a block or a hazard each time its red button is pressed (Chapter 9). Chapter 10, the last, brings in nothing new: its three teaching levels each join two earlier pieces in a new way (the wooden cow luring the beast into a snare, Medusa's mirror stilling him while Talos's fire runs on, a block pushed through a teleport onto a bomb), and its last level ends the story at the middle of the maze. The solver proves each level winnable. Nine trap and trick pieces from Chip's Challenge are in the rules and both solvers too (Pieces, below); all nine are in released levels (the pace is in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-29-crete-chapter-2-design.md`). The campaign is ten chapters, all released; Chapter 10's closing card ends it with one of three endings, chosen by the story pages found across the campaign (every one, some or none).

The page is one frame: a status bar (the level, as "Ch. 1 • Level 3/10" with its name in the tooltip, on a portrait phone on a row of its own; Hearts, Relics, Keys, Gear, Moves, Best, Menu, each number in a quiet colour of its own (Hearts soft red, Relics gold, Keys their door colours, Gear teal, Moves plain), the labels dim; Best is the level's fewest possible moves where they are known (the campaign's table, an Endless level's search, a try's or a shared level's proven line), soft green while a perfect clear is possible and, once Moves passes it, amber with how many over (`10 +3`), clearing again when Moves drops back; its tooltip adds the player's own best clear; on a portrait phone Menu moves to the strip's bottom row, between Hints and the Map button), the board, and a control strip (the move pad, one connected cross of four arms round an inert centre square showing Theseus's face (`fx/sprites/theseus-face.png`, cut from his sprite); a press only arms the arrow it lands on: lifted there it is a tap, one move, and held 300 ms it moves, then every 150 ms once the move before has played; a finger slid into another arrow moves that way at once (the arrow it landed on never moves), walks on while held there and stops over the centre; the lift is heard anywhere on the page, and the click a press makes is never a second move (`src/pad.ts`); Wait, Undo, Restart, Hint, Route, Auto, the three tools with their counts and the Map button), each button with a key tab. On a phone Hint, Route and Auto sit behind one Hints button, whose panel opens on a tap and shuts on a choice, a second tap, a tap elsewhere or Q (the focus goes back to Hints, except after a tap elsewhere); Hints shows on while the route is drawn or the play-out runs. Arrows or WASD move, `.` waits, U undoes, R restarts the level, H plays one step of Ariadne's thread, V draws all of it, T plays it out, Z/X/C use the tools, M mutes, N opens the level list, E on the title card opens the level editor, and Q opens the menu and closes whatever is open. Escape only puts away a rule tip and backs out of a card opened from the title card, as Q does (Cards, Q and settings, below). Any use of the thread marks the try assisted; this browser keeps which levels are cleared and the fewest moves of an unassisted clear. Every campaign level's fewest possible moves (the solver's shortest winning line, kept in `src/campaign/fewest.ts` and checked against the solver by `test/fewest.test.ts`) shows in the level list and on the ending card, which sets a win against it: `best possible 12 (2 over)`, or `perfect` at the fewest. The level list colours its marks as the status bar does, each still in words: `cleared` soft green with a tick, `perfect` gold with a star, `(2 over)` amber, `page found` parchment, and a level not yet played plain. A turn that brings the whole situation back to an earlier state of the try (Theseus, the Minotaur, the hazards, the blocks, what is held and taken, the doors and walls, the hearts: everything but the move count) is an undo to there: the turns between go as if Undo had been pressed that many times, Moves drops to that state's count, and a toast says `↶ Back to move 12`. So stepping back the way he came, when nothing else moved, takes those moves back, and a Wait in which nothing moves is no turn; the fewest and the best a win records use the count as it stands (`src/puzzle/run.ts`). The classic board shows the whole level at the start on every screen, however small its cells, and turns and tilts like the modern one (Classic, below). It zooms in, up to 4× or a 64 px cell, whichever is less: two fingers pinch, the wheel zooms toward the pointer, and + and − zoom while the compass has the focus; the board has no camera buttons. Zoomed in, the view pans over the board: a step never changes the zoom, the view pans to keep Theseus two cells inside it, and two fingers or the right or middle button drag it. A double tap or double click, Reset and a new level go back to the opening view, Restart to the whole level; Undo keeps the zoom. A story page is found by taking it and winning that level while holding it. The first time Theseus picks up a kind of item, or touches an active tile (the toggle, brown and red buttons, a teleport, the thief, a snare that holds him, a pop-up wall rising behind him, a bomb, a fake or invisible wall he finds, the open Daedalus Gate he walks through), play waits on a first card with its picture in the board's look, its name, a line of lore and what it does and how to use it; every later pickup or touch slides in a banner (picture, name, a reminder, the tool's key) that fades after 2.5 s and blocks nothing. Nothing else interrupts play: a level opens on its start card alone, a story page opens only its story, and the Minotaur, the moving hazards and the passive tiles have no card. The menu's Dictionary (G) has two tabs: Backpack, what Theseus holds in this try with counts, and Entries, every item and tile, items locked as a silhouette named `???` until first picked up (First meetings and the Dictionary, below). A `?seed=` link (any value, with or without `&tier=`) skips the title card and opens the Endless card on that seed (Endless, below).

It is a `play` game: the folder is a complete page (`index.html`) with its own PWA files, and the site wraps it in `play/crete/` without the game knowing.

## Build

`index.html` loads `dist/main.js` with `<script type="module">`. The patterns shared with the other games are in `../_kit/README.md`.

```
cd .. && npm ci                            # once, in games/
node ../_kit/build/build.mjs crete         # type-check src/ and bundle into dist/
node ../_kit/build/build.mjs crete --check # fail if dist/ is missing or stale
php tools/install-files.php                # then rewrite the ?v= versions and sw.js
```

- `dist/` is generated: edit `src/`, rebuild, rerun `install-files.php`, commit all of it. `make test` runs `npm ci` and `--check`, and `deploy.sh` refuses a stale `dist/`.
- `tsconfig.json` extends the kit's base, is strict and lists the DOM-free modules, which compile without page types, so they cannot reach for `document`. `src/tsconfig.dom.json` extends it with the DOM lib for `audio.ts`, `music-decks.ts`, `ui.ts`, the views, `board.ts`, `main.ts` and `solver-client.ts`, and `src/tsconfig.worker.json` with the WebWorker lib for `solver-worker.ts`. Imports carry their `.ts` extension.
- Versions are content hashes written by `tools/install-files.php`, never by hand: `dist/main.js?v=` and `game.css?v=` in `index.html`. The solver worker's `?v=` is its own bundle's hash, which the build puts into `dist/main.js` (`SOLVER_WORKER_V` in `build.json`'s `versions`). `sw.js` hashes the same files plus the rest of `dist/` and `fx/sprites/`, so a changed bundle or atlas makes it stale too.

## The seam

The rules never name the page. `game.ts` takes everything it needs as `GameDeps` (`sfx`, `say`, `progress`, `pages`, `levels`, `timers`, `clock`, `reducedMotion`, `changed`, and `isFirst`, which says which meetings are firsts), so `test/game.test.ts` plays whole levels against recording hooks and a manual clock. `ui.ts` and `audio.ts` are the other ends of those hooks.

A turn that animates draws the state *before* it while the player's figure walks, less each thing the figure has reached (`frameOf` in `game.ts`; the timeline's `taken` gives the moment it lands on each), then the state once the player's action alone has played (`act` in `puzzle/step.ts`, kept as `anim.mid`) while the hazards move, and the state after the turn once the hazards' moves land (the timeline's `hazardEnd`), while the Minotaur steps. Hazards come from the timeline's figures, not the state: the hazards before the turn, moving all at once after the player and before the Minotaur, and the hazards after it once the turn lands, so a hazard destroyed or sent home plays its move and is gone or home only then, and a cloned one appears then, at `hazardEnd` like a cloned block, before the Minotaur steps. Through a teleport a figure jumps to the exit and steps on. The animation pauses the moment the figure reaches a story page, or first meets an item or an active tile (`game.animMs()` stops there and sets `anim.paused` to the stop, `kind` `page` or `meet`), and holds that frame while the page shows its card; `game.resume()` goes on from the same moment, so a slide over several pages stops at each in turn. A play-out waits while its turn has a page still to reach. A key during a slide finishes it and acts at once, exactly once. Reduced motion is read live from `prefers-reduced-motion` and snaps the figures. The thread solves with a cap of 200,000 states; when none is found in that, or the try cannot be won, the toast says the thread goes slack and to undo or restart.

## Pieces

The trap and trick pieces, by their character in the level text. The code uses the plain name; the game and the help text use the myth name where there is one.

| Char | Plain name | In the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `&` | `trap` | Daedalus's snare |
| `-` | `button-brown` | Brown button |
| `+` | `button-red` | Red button |
| `;` | `popup` | Pop-up wall |
| `:` | `gravel` | Gravel |
| `W` | `fake-wall` | Fake wall |
| `I` | `invisible-wall` | Invisible wall |
| `@` | `teleport` | Teleport |
| `$` | `thief` | Thief |
| `!` | `bomb` | Bomb |
| `C` | `clone` | Clone machine |
| `o` | `ball` | Bronze ball |
| `w` | `glider` | Harpy |
| `e` | `fireball` | Talos's fire |

A hazard (`o`, `w`, `e`) stands on floor; a clone machine's cell is terrain `clone` with nothing on it. Two headers go with them, each a `LevelError` at the header when its count or a word is wrong:

- `facing:` one direction (`up`, `down`, `left`, `right`) per hazard on the grid, in reading order; required exactly when the grid has a hazard.
- `machines:` one `<template>-<dir>` per `C`, in reading order, the template `block`, `ball`, `glider` or `fireball`; required exactly when the grid has a `C`.

Links are never written. As in Chip's Challenge, each brown button controls the first snare after it in reading order, wrapping to the first, and each red button the first clone machine after it; a button with nothing to control does nothing. `parse` derives `Level.links`.

- **Daedalus's snare**: whoever stands on it (Theseus, a block, a hazard, the Minotaur) is held while no linked brown button is held down by Theseus, a block or a hazard (never the Minotaur or the cow). Theseus's move while held is a bump; waiting and tools are turns. A block in a held snare cannot be pushed. The snare keeps no state of its own: it holds or not by what stands on its buttons now.
- **Pop-up wall**: floor until Theseus leaves it by any means (a step, a slide, a throw, a teleport), then wall. Nothing else enters it.
- **Gravel**: Theseus crosses it; blocks, the Minotaur and hazards do not.
- **Fake wall**: wall to everyone until Theseus bumps it, which opens it, and he steps through on that same move. Standing still beside one shows a faint haze on it (Hidden walls, in The looks).
- **Invisible wall**: looks like floor and is wall. While Theseus stands beside one not yet shown, a pane of its glass shows on its face toward him, gone once he is not beside it. His bump shows it and costs a turn: he stays put while the hazards and the Minotaur move, as on a Wait. It shimmers into view and stays marked as found glass while the state shows it (Hidden walls, in The looks). Slides, pushed blocks and teleport exits reveal neither kind.
- **The start card's hint**: a level with a fake wall, an invisible wall or both carries one quiet line under its intro on its start card, in the Labyrinth's words and never saying where (`src/wall-hint.ts`: three lines for each case, the line a level gets picked by a hash of its grid, so a campaign level, an Endless seed, a shared level and the editor's Try It always show the same one). Chapter 4 has none, since each of its intros names its painted and unseen walls.
- **Teleport**: Theseus or a block comes out of the next teleport in reading order (wrapping), onto the cell beyond it in the same direction, and arrives there in full; a blocked exit is skipped for the next. Wall to the Minotaur and hazards; a lone teleport is wall.
- **Thief**: takes all of Theseus's gear; keys and tools stay.
- **Bomb**: a block or a hazard arriving on it is destroyed with it; Theseus arriving on it loses a heart and is thrown back as in a catch, and the bomb is gone. Wall to the Minotaur.
- **Clone machine**: wall to everyone. Each time something arrives on its red button it makes its template in front of it, facing its way, when that cell is free.
- **Bronze ball**: goes straight; turns back when blocked. **Harpy**: turns left, then right, then back; crosses water. **Talos's fire**: turns right, then left, then back; crosses fire. A hazard stuck every way stays. A hazard keeps to the ground the Minotaur keeps to, and a bomb, which destroys it; ice and force floors carry it as they carry him. It never enters a cell with an object, a block, the cow, the Minotaur or another hazard. Theseus stepping into a hazard, or a hazard into him, is a catch. The Minotaur and a hazard never share a cell, stone or not.

A turn goes in a fixed order and stops at its first catch: Theseus's action, then each hazard once, in reading order of the cells they stood on when the phase began (a hazard that moves keeps its place in that order), then the Minotaur's two steps.

The rulings the pieces need:

- **Sent home**: when a catch throws Theseus onto a hazard, or the Minotaur home onto one, that hazard goes back to its start (a level hazard's own cell; a clone's, the cell in front of its machine). If that is taken, it goes to the nearest cell it may stand on, breadth-first from its start over every cell, ties in reading order, never a bomb; only when no cell on the board can take it is it removed. Being set down is no arrival: it presses no button, though a snare it is set down in holds it.
- **The cow**: a hazard never enters the cow's cell, but one that moves onto Theseus standing on his cow catches him and the cow is lost. The cow never holds a brown button down.
- **Hazards and the toggle button**: a hazard on `_` flips the toggle walls, as Theseus and blocks do.
- **Teleport chains**: there are none. A teleport beyond an exit makes that exit blocked. Out of a teleport, Theseus pushes a block only when he entered it by his own step, not on a ride; with every exit blocked he stays on the teleport he entered, and a pushed block's push is refused.
- **Bomb catches**: a bomb under Theseus is a catch (`caught` with `by: 'bomb'`), so a search that refuses catches refuses it and the turn stops there. A hazard's catch is `by: 'hazard'`, the Minotaur's `by: 'minotaur'`.
- **Clones arrive in full**: a cloned block settles like a pushed one (it sinks, explodes, presses, slides); a cloned hazard presses buttons and meets a bomb on its cell, and first moves in the next hazard phase. One made during the hazard phase joins the end of the list and does not move until the next phase. A red button pressed by a clone's arrival fires again; each firing needs a free cell, so a chain ends.
- **No clone on the cell Theseus enters**: a clone is never made on Theseus, nor on the cell he is stepping into while his own push presses the button.

## The solver

`solve` in `src/puzzle/solver.ts` is exact: it returns a shortest winning line, proves a level unwinnable, or says it gave up at its cap (2,000,000 stored situations; capped is never "no win"). It is A\* (`astar.ts`) over situations packed into a few 32-bit words (`pack.ts`). A situation holds everything that can change during a try and nothing else: the cells of Theseus, the Minotaur, the cow and the throw-back, the Minotaur's stone turns, hearts, relics still needed, story pages, keys, tools and gear, one bit for each cell whose terrain changes once (a door or fake wall opens, water fills, a pop-up rises, an invisible wall shows, a bomb goes off) and one for the toggle walls, the objects and blocks where they lie, and each hazard's cell, facing and home (the home decides its kind). Where no machine clones a hazard the hazards pack as one slot per level hazard; otherwise per cell a hazard could ever stand on. A snare holds no state: whether it holds is read from its buttons. The search runs on that, with an estimate that never overstates the moves left (`heuristic.ts`) and pruning that drops a situation another one already seen beats on every resource that only helps (`prune.ts`). A move into an invisible wall not yet shown is a turn that plays exactly as a Wait (the wall it shows is wall to everyone already), so the searches, the difficulty measure and the rating leave it out and try the Wait (`searchSkips` in `step.ts`); the breadth-first reference solver in the tests plays it as a turn, and the two agree. The nodes live in typed arrays (`store.ts`); a spilling store keeps the first ones in memory and the rest in fixed-size records on pages of a file (`paged.ts`), and a file that fails ends the search capped with the failure as its reason. `searching` runs the same search in batches, yielding its progress between them; `measure` has `measuring` the same way, its catch search (`catching`, a breadth-first search with no estimate, so its progress reports a best f and start estimate of 0) included. The game's thread help solves in the page, as it always answers within a few hundred milliseconds on Chapter 1's levels.

The search's hot path copies as little as it can. A turn (`step`) works on a copy of the state (`turnOf` in `work.ts`) that shares the state's terrain, objects, keys, tools and gear until the turn changes one of them (`setTerrain`, `setObject`, `own`), so most turns copy no board at all; the state it returns shares whatever the turn left as it was. `unpack` hands every situation with the same terrain bits the same terrain array, and the same for the objects (kept per level, 4,096 of each at most), and shares one cell object per cell; `pack` given the situation a child was played from copies the parent's packed terrain and objects bits when the child shares those arrays. Cells come from one table (`cellAt` in `state.ts`), so a step makes none. Nothing ever writes to a state's arrays: a turn copies before it writes, and `work(s)` gives callers that edit in place copies of their own. `test/engine-same-0.test.ts` to `-3` hold the engine to the answers it gave before this was so, a quarter each: every campaign level's measure (fewest moves, line, situations stored, every other field), every-piece verdicts and threat, every bank level's measure and gate, and ten Endless searches with the levels they make and the situations they spend (`test/engine-same/shard-<k>.json`, recorded by `tools/engine-same.ts record <k>`; a new or rebuilt campaign or bank level is recorded the same way). Any later change to the engine's speed must keep them, bit for bit.

The editor and any long search run in a Web Worker instead. `startSearch(levelText, kind)` in `src/solver-client.ts` (kind `solve` or `measure`) returns a job: `progress(cb)` (expansions, situations stored, the best f, the start's estimate, and `storage`), `stop()`, `resume()`, `cancel()` and `result`. One worker serves every search; a new search cancels the one before it, stopped or not, whose result rejects with the host's message. `cancel()` ends a job for good: `result` rejects with `CancelledError` at once, no more progress arrives, and the host closes the job's node file like a replaced search's. A worker that errors (it failed to load, or threw) is terminated and its searches reject, and the next search starts a fresh one; where no worker can be made, the search rejects at once. In the worker (`src/solver-host.ts`):

- A finished result (solved, or proved unwinnable; for a measure, nothing unknown) is cached under the kind, the solver's version and the SHA-256 of the exact level text, in the origin private file system (`crete-results/`), and a second search of the same text answers from it with no progress. The version is a hash of the worker's own bundle, which holds every rule and the solver, so a new build never serves an old build's answer; when the bundle cannot be read, nothing is cached. After each put the cache is trimmed (`evictions` in `src/puzzle/cache.ts`): entries of another version go first, then all but the newest `RESULT_LIMIT = 256` by file `lastModified`. A cached line is served only if it still wins the level on its last move, and a cached measure with no `line` is searched again. Any storage error reads as a miss; nothing throws to the page.
- The search runs 4,096 expansions per batch, posts its progress, then lets messages in: stop waits there until resume, which goes on with the same search.
- Its nodes past the first 65,536 go to a node file in the origin private file system (`crete-nodes/`), written through a sync access handle in 64 KiB pages of which 64 (4 MiB) are cached. The file's size is the handle's, and a short read or write throws, so the search ends capped with a reason. When the file system is missing or refuses (a private window, no quota), the nodes stay in memory and progress says `storage: 'memory'`. The file is closed and deleted when the search ends or is cancelled; one a closed tab left behind is deleted by the first search six hours after it was made.
- Memory is bounded before the cap: per stored node, the A\* arrays and heap take 19 bytes, pruning's index at most 8 × words + 20, and the store 12 bytes with the file (its hash and table) or 4 × words + 21 without; every array grows by doubling, so to at most twice that. With the file the 65,536 nodes in memory (4 × words + 9 bytes each) and the page cache come on top. The worker's cap is the solver's 2,000,000 or less, so that the total stays within 384 MiB: Chapter 1's levels (3 words) and anything up to 5 words keep 2,000,000 with the file; 10 words get 1,508,578, and a level that packs into 23 words 833,701. Should memory run out below that anyway, a solve ends capped with the failure as its reason and a measure rejects with it. The disk holds only the nodes themselves: pruning's index, the A\* arrays, the heap and the store's hash stay in memory, so the file extends a search's reach by a constant factor, not without bound.

`test/solver-big.test.ts` proves a generated 40 × 40 level (keys, doors, two blocks, a Minotaur; 145 moves) in about a second and checks it against the reference.

## What makes a good level

Winnable is not enough: a level the solver wins in a few hundred situations, with the Minotaur walled off, is no fight. `measure` in `src/puzzle/difficulty.ts` runs the solver three ways and reports the shortest line (`line`, as move letters; `''` when unsolved, so Check holds the proof a share link carries) and the situations stored to find it (`explored`: the A\* search with pruning in `src/puzzle/solver.ts`), the shortest line with the Minotaur taken off the board (`minotaurDelta` is how many moves he adds), whether that Minotaur-free line still wins when played on the real level (`greedyWins`), the shortest line that is never caught, and the catches on the shortest line. A search that hits its cap reports unknown, never none. `hardPieces(level)` names the pieces that make a level hard ("4 blocks", "2 keys and 2 doors"), the most numerous first.

It also labels the level and plays the plain walk:

- **chase** or **solo** (`label`): `canCatch` searches breadth-first from the start and stops at the first turn the Minotaur catches Theseus. A catch by him makes the level chase; a hazard's or a bomb's never does. A level with no Minotaur, or one whose search ends with no catch by him (he taunts Theseus from behind walls or water), is solo; when the cells the Minotaur could ever reach and the cells Theseus could ever reach do not meet, that is proved without a search (a door counts open only when its key is on the board, water only when a block or a block machine could fill it; a fake wall or a bomb counts open to the Minotaur; Theseus goes through every piece but a wall, and from any teleport to every cell beside any teleport). A capped search labels it unknown. The search yields its progress in batches like the others, so the worker can report and stop it.
- **The plain walk** (`naiveWins`): the shortest path from Theseus to the exit over plain floor, ignoring every piece (only walls stop it) and taking the relics the gate needs on the way, played as moves on the real level. `naiveWins` is true when that walk wins, false when it fails or there is no plain path.

`challengeProblems` checks a measure against `BAR`, the campaign's bar, and lists what falls short:

- Every level with a Minotaur: he shapes the route. He adds moves, or the line that ignores him loses.
- Every level: no catch is needed (`catchProblems`). The shortest line takes no catch, and the shortest line that never takes one is as short.
- A chapter's challenge levels (those after its teaching stretch, `teaching` in `src/campaign/chapters.ts`; Chapter 1 teaches in levels 1 to 8, Chapters 2 to 5 in levels 1 to 3) are a real fight: the line that ignores the Minotaur loses, he adds at least 8 moves, the search stores at least 20 times the situations of the chapter's hardest teaching level, and the line is no shorter than its longest teaching level's. `rampProblems` holds each challenge level after the first to between `RAMP.least` and `RAMP.most` (1.2 and 2.2) times the situations of the one before it, a steady climb with no cliff; `rampOf(level)` gives a level that hides walls the gentler `HIDDEN.ramp` instead.
- A challenge level that hides walls (a fake or an invisible wall) stays at or under `HIDDEN.most`, 20,000 situations (`hiddenProblems`). `measure` sees through hidden walls and a player has to find them, so such a level gets an easier maze than a plain level at the same spot.

`sharedProblems` checks a measure against `SHARED_BAR`, the bar every shared level must clear. It never needs a Minotaur, so a solo level clears it on puzzle difficulty alone:

- The level is proved winnable, the plain walk does not win, and no catch is needed (`catchProblems`).
- The quickest win takes at least 40 moves, and the solver stores at least 12,000 situations to find it.
- A chase level whose Minotaur adds at least 8 moves (`BAR.minotaurDelta`), and whose line that ignores him loses, needs only 30 moves and 6,000 situations.

The shared bar sits a little under level 9's situations (12,000 against 16,596) and its 43 moves (40), a few past the longest teaching level (36), so levels 9 and 10 clear it and levels 1 to 8 fall far short. `test/difficulty.test.ts` gates every released level on both bars (`test/chapter-2.test.ts` checks Chapter 2's own rules: gravel and nothing later, the teaching levels' gravel, the pages, level 10's water and fire); new campaign levels must pass `BAR` and every shared level `SHARED_BAR`.

### Rules for new levels

Any session or agent that makes or changes campaign levels keeps to these:

- **The gate.** Every teaching level passes the base `challengeProblems` check, with no context: its Minotaur shapes the route (he adds moves, or the line that ignores him loses). Every challenge level passes `challengeProblems` against `BAR` with the chapter's teaching levels as context: the line that ignores the Minotaur loses, he adds at least 8 moves, the search stores at least 20 times the situations of the chapter's hardest teaching level, and the line is no shorter than the longest teaching level's. Every challenge level also clears `SHARED_BAR`.
- **No catch is ever needed.** A player cannot know to walk into the beast, so no level, teaching or challenge, campaign or shared, needs a catch: not to win, and not for its fewest moves. The solver's shortest line takes no catch and no line that takes one is shorter than the best catch-free line (`catchProblems`, part of both `challengeProblems` and `sharedProblems`). A catch still costs a heart and wakes Theseus on his last safe mark; it is a setback, never a route.
- **Every piece has a purpose a player sees.** No level holds a piece the player can walk past (`purposeProblems`, checked on every campaign level in `test/difficulty.test.ts`, and on every Endless level). A piece, or a linked pair (a colour's keys and doors), has a purpose when the solver's shortest line uses it (walks through a door of the key's colour, wears the gear on its ground, raises the mirror, sets down the cow, takes a spare relic, pushes a block, presses a button, crosses a tile, holds the beast in a snare), or when taking it out makes the level unwinnable or changes its fewest moves (a door that holds the beast back). Each piece is taken out every way it can be (`pieceUnits`): what Theseus would take is gone (a colour's keys, gear, the mirror, the cow, a spare relic); what he would open, push or cross is tried both shut, as wall, and gone, as floor (a colour's doors, taken with its keys as a pair, a block, a fake wall, water, fire); gravel, marked stones and pop-up walls are tried as floor and as wall; ice and currents as floor and without their gear; a snare goes with its brown buttons, and the toggle walls with their button; a moving hazard is taken off the board. A piece that only makes the search bigger, a door and key off the quick way, is a decoy and fails. Floor kinds count as one piece each (all the gravel). A honey cake and a story page are never counted: a cake is a spare heart and no level needs a catch, and a page is a reward off the quick way.
- **The beast is a real threat.** He is never penned from the start without Theseus working for it (`threatProblems`, in `test/difficulty.test.ts`). Every level's beast adds at least a move: no route that ignores him wins as quickly. On the solver's shortest line a turn is live when, after it, the beast is within `THREAT.reach` (8) steps of Theseus over ground he may tread (four of his turns), or is stone or held in a snare, which Theseus worked for. A challenge level's beast is live on at least a third of the turns and never quiet for more than a third of them in a row. A teaching level shows one piece and may leave the beast behind once it has: a quarter of the turns, with no limit on a quiet stretch. Two teaching levels whose lesson is keeping him away, Chapter 3's "Stones He Will Not Tread" and Chapter 5's "The Brown Button", are exempt from the share, by id in `THREAT_EXEMPT`; the test fails if either stops needing it.
- **The ramp.** Each challenge level stores 1.2 to 2.2 times the situations of the one before it (`RAMP`); aim near 1.3 times, so that no level is a wall that makes players quit.
- **Hidden walls ease the level.** A level with a fake or an invisible wall climbs by `HIDDEN.ramp` (1.1 to 1.5 times) and stays at or under `HIDDEN.most` (20,000 situations). A step into a fake wall opens it and walks Theseus through as one move; bumping an invisible wall shows it and costs a turn, as a Wait does.
- **The finale.** A chapter's last level sits a little above the previous chapter's last level, unless it hides walls and so is held under `HIDDEN.most`.
- **Teach, then mix.** A chapter's first levels (`teaching` in `src/campaign/chapters.ts`) each teach its new piece alone, and its chapter test checks that each one needs that piece; the challenge levels then mix it with the earlier pieces.
- **Tidy layouts.** Levels read as rooms and halls (Endless's as corridor mazes), never as scattered noise; a tuning search that moves walls keeps the rooms.
- **New layouts.** Every level is drawn new, never an earlier level turned, flipped or lightly edited: players notice repeats. `test/layouts.test.ts` compares each released level with every other, under all eight turns and flips and at every offset when the sizes differ by at most two rows and columns. It compares features: every cell that is not plain floor, by kind (walls with the exit and gate, ice, water, fire, gravel, force floors whichever way they run, teleports and the rest), leaving out filler walls with wall all round them. The overlap is the features two levels share, cell for cell and kind for kind, over the cells where either has one, and the test fails at 0.42: levels grown from others measured 0.44 to 0.96 against their sources, levels drawn apart at most 0.40. Matching cells would be no measure: floor is most of every level, so unrelated levels agree on 70 to 85 per cent of their cells. Two teaching levels are not compared with each other, since each is a small board of a hall or two built to show one piece.
- **Real quotes.** Chapter cards, intros and story pages quote the public-domain translations the vision spec lists, word for word with a citation; the chapters so far quote Plutarch's *Life of Theseus* in Bernadotte Perrin's 1914 translation, Ovid's *Metamorphoses* in Brookes More's 1922 translation, Apollodorus's *Library* in J. G. Frazer's 1921 translation, Apollonius of Rhodes's *Argonautica* in R. C. Seaton's 1912 translation, Hesiod's *Theogony* and the Homeric Hymn to Hermes in H. G. Evelyn-White's 1914 translation, and Catullus's poem 64 in Leonard C. Smithers's 1894 prose translation; Nathaniel Hawthorne's *Tanglewood Tales* (1853), written in English, is quoted as he wrote it, cited by its year with no translator.
- **Fewest moves.** Every level change regenerates its entry in `src/campaign/fewest.ts` from the solver; `test/fewest.test.ts` checks every entry.
- **One gate for every tool.** Anything that makes or tunes levels (a tuning script, the Endless generator) accepts a level only through the checks the tests use: `challengeProblems` (which carries `catchProblems`), `purposeProblems`, `threatProblems` (with the teaching or challenge bar), `rampProblems` with `rampOf`, `hiddenProblems`, and `sharedProblems` against `SHARED_BAR` for a challenge level, all in `src/puzzle/difficulty.ts`. Nothing starts under a figure (`startProblems`). The Endless generator applies every one of them, the two for pieces and the beast included, to each level it makes (`levelChecks` and `gateProblems` in `src/endless/gate.ts`), reading the situations bars at its own floor and climbing by its own score (Endless, below), with the rules of `src/puzzle/rating.ts` (no habit player wins, open space earns its place, a cow or mirror is needed) and the tidy and new-layout rules as checks of their own; the editor's Check rates a level by the same `levelChecks`. The rules live in those checks, not in each tool. The campaign is measured against the habit and openness rules but not yet held to them (`test/difficulty.test.ts` reports which levels a habit wins and which are over the open ceiling).

## The looks

The look is picked on the new-game card after New Game (two pictures from `fx/looks/`, the same 8 × 6 squares of one level in each look, framed as the Map button frames them; `make screenshots GAMES=crete` retakes them with the game's other pictures) and swapped during play with the strip's Map button (B), so the menu has no Look setting. The Map button's face shows the look it swaps to. While Modern shows it is a live crop of 7 × 5 squares round Theseus in the Classic tiles (drawn north up with `src/board.ts`), labelled Map, repainted only once a move has landed, never per frame; while Classic shows it is the Modern look's picture from the new-game card (`fx/looks/modern.jpg`), labelled 3D, so Classic never loads the 3D board. The heading and tilt go across with the switch, both ways. Its accessible name is Switch to Classic or Switch to Modern. Theseus plays on in either look. The look is stored as `crete-look` (`modern` or `classic`, default `modern`); Walls is stored as `crete-walls` (`solid`, `ghosted` or `low`, default `ghosted`) and shows in the menu in Modern only.

- **Classic** is the Blender sprites on a tilted sheet, with no 3D graphics, so it stays light on any computer. The terrain and Ariadne's thread are drawn on the `#stage` canvas (`src/sheet-board.ts`), which a CSS transform from `src/sheet.ts` turns, tilts (with a gentle lens) and places; the figures, items and hazards stand upright over it on the `.stage-figures` canvas at their sprites' own pixels, each on a soft shadow on its square's projected centre, nearest drawn last. A raised tile (a wall, a door, a closed toggle wall) shows its top leaning away from the viewer and its face toward them: the sheet draws each tile turned to the quarter of the view nearest the heading, farthest row first, and a tile whose picture points along the grid (a force floor, a clone machine) keeps its own way round. Each canvas repaints only when what it shows has changed. Classic shares the camera's heading and tilt (Camera, below): it opens at 75° and north up, turns to any heading and tilts from straight down to 10° under the same gestures, buttons and compass (`#classic-compass`, the same widget as Modern's, `createCompassButton` in `src/modern/controls.ts`), and eases where a button, a key or the compass sends it. While a whole level shows, a turn scales the sheet to keep the level in view; once the view rests, the sheet is drawn again at the cell that fits it unscaled.
- **Modern** is `#stage3d`, a three.js scene (`src/modern/`): flagstone floor, walls, a model for every piece and figure, torch and lamp light, then a crisp finish: a tone map, a light warm grade and a vignette, anti-aliased (four samples a pixel for the scene, then FXAA); no pass blurs or repaints the picture. The figures wear a thin dark rim that parts them from the floor, as Classic's sprites are outlined. Theseus stands out most: brighter, faintly glowing colours, a little larger than the others, on a green ring with a dark edge in his marker's colour. Walls and wall tops are cool grey stone with a dark outline on each wall top's open edge, floors pale warm sand in wide flagstones over light mortar, with a soft shade at each wall's foot, and a near-black rim round the board. Models are built in code from primitives and procedural textures; the sprite atlas only supplies the mini map. The board is drawn only when something changes (a move, the camera easing, a wall fading, a figure turning) and the tile loops (water ripples, fire, flames, discs, torches, the floating markers' bob and the floating block's bob) tick at 12 fps, only while one is on screen, the tab is visible and motion is not reduced. At rest it draws nothing. It draws at the device's pixel ratio (at most 2). A render quality ladder measures the frame rate of animating frames only (idle waits, stalls and the first frames after a level opens or the tab returns are left out): under 40 fps over two seconds of animation it drops shadows, then FXAA, then the pixel ratio to 1, one step at a time; over 55 fps for three seconds it steps back up, unless that step has proved too slow twice.
- **Walls**: Solid, Ghosted (a wall that hides Theseus, or any piece or figure, fades to a third) and Low (walls a third as high). Theseus is never hidden behind a wall in any mode. Walls never hide the markers.
- **Items**: every object Theseus can take and every hazard is sized to fill about 0.8 of its cell, as Classic's sprites do, so each can be named at the whole-level view on a phone; it wears the figures' thin dark rim, has bright, faintly glowing colours and stands on a dark disc that parts it from the pale floor. An object stands on a plate: the disc with a thin cream ring at its edge. A relic, a key, the mirror, boots, the scroll and the cow lean back toward the camera so their faces show from above; a key's bow and gem are in its door's colour. Reduced motion switches it at once.
- **Hidden walls** (`src/wall-cues.ts`, shared by both looks; only how they look, never the rules): an invisible wall that Theseus has found wears a faint cool sheen of glass with a pale edge, so it reads as found among the stone; as he bumps it, it shimmers into view over `SHIMMER_MS` (a flash and a band of light sweeping across it), then settles. Which walls are found is read from the state (the level's invisible walls that are wall now), so it shows the same in either look, across any number of look switches, and an Undo of the bump or a restart hides it again; a wall already found when a look first draws the level settles at once. An invisible wall not yet shown, while Theseus stands beside it, shows a pane of pale glass on its face toward him, about a quarter of a cell deep, just inside its cell and the wall's height, coming up over `GLASS_IN_MS` (at once under reduced motion) as his figure arrives and gone the moment he is not beside it; it is read from where he stands and never kept, so the player has to remember it. A fake wall beside Theseus, once nothing has changed in the try for `IDLE_MS` (2.5 s) with no card open, shows a faint warm haze on its face toward him that comes up and breathes gently while he stays (`hazeStrength`), and goes the moment a move, an undo or a restart lands; it changes nothing in the try. Classic draws them on the sheet in 2D (`src/sheet-board.ts`), each with its tile, so a nearer tile still covers it: the sheen and edge over the tile, the shimmer's band as a gradient, the glass as a pale cool band along that side with a bright line at its inner edge, the haze as a tint and three faint wavering lines along that side. Modern draws them in the scene (`src/modern/scene/wall-cues.ts`): a glass shell a hair larger than the wall, brighter at its edges, with a shader band for the shimmer; the glass beside Theseus as a slab of pale cool glass, paler at its edges, blended over the floor (not added, so it shows on the pale sand) along the face toward him; and a sheet just off the face toward him plus a strip along the top's edge on that side, faint warm bands wavering like heat over stone. Under reduced motion there is no shimmer, the glass shows at once and the haze is a still tint.
- **Item bubble** (`src/item-bubble.ts`): while the Minotaur stands on an item (a relic, a key, gear, a tool or a story page), a small thought bubble up and to the right of his head shows it, with two puffs down to the head: in Classic the item's sprite, in Modern its model on its plate from the Dictionary's icons (`fx/sprites/modern-icons.webp`). It shows only once he has landed on the cell, never mid-step, and goes when he steps off. Theseus never has one, since he takes whatever he steps on, and the hazards never stand on an item (the rules keep them off objects). The bubble is about three quarters of a cell across, 34 to 54 CSS px. Where the board's edge would cut it off, as on the top row of a level that fills the board, it goes up and to the left, then to either side, then below. It is a DOM layer over each look's board (`.item-bubbles`, under the compass and the mini map), placed from where that look shows his head at any heading and tilt; it fades in and out in 160 ms, at once under reduced motion.
- **Floating markers**: a small glass crystal, point down, with a thin dark rim, floats over Theseus (green) and the Minotaur (red, stone or not). It hangs just above his head as the screen shows it, so it sits over him however the view tilts (at the opening 75° its point stays inside his cell), over the walls, bobbing gently (still when motion is reduced), never under 10 CSS px across however far out the view is; while its figure is on screen a marker slides inside the board's edge rather than being cut off. Markers are decoration: they take no taps and say nothing to a screen reader.
- **Switchable choices**: `src/modern/variants.ts` holds the look's switches and what ships: items on plates (or a cyan or white crystal over each object and a violet one over each hazard), and, all off, a beacon over Theseus with his outline through walls, cut-away walls, pale wall tops with dark floor joints. A browser can override them for sample renders with the `crete-variant` key (JSON).
- **Blocks in water**: a block pushed into water fills it (the rules make the cell dirt); the modern board draws the cell as the block floating in the water, its top just above the surface, bobbing slowly. Going in, it splashes, dips under and rises to float; under reduced motion it simply floats. Undo or a restart empties the water again.
- **Camera**: one camera, always free to turn and tilt (there is no view setting). Every level opens at a 75° pitch and north up, square to the board like the classic look, so the whole maze shows and a wall hides only about a quarter of the cell behind it. The tilt runs anywhere from straight down (90°) to 10°, nearly edge-on, and no further either way; low down, a wall hides what stands up to seven cells behind it, so a wall in front of Theseus or another figure fades, and the camera stands back far enough (`cameraDistance` in `src/modern/camera.ts`) that the near ground is never cut off; the heading turns freely to any angle. Whatever heading and tilt the player leaves the view at, it stays there: nothing squares it up. One finger, like the left mouse button, drags across to turn and up and down to tilt; two fingers drag to pan, twist to turn and pinch to zoom about themselves (`src/modern/gestures.ts`, which the classic board reads too). A finger on the board never moves Theseus: the move pad does. The right or middle button pans, with no context menu; the wheel zooms toward the pointer, from about five rows in to the whole level turned any way out, however big it is. The arrows and the pad move Theseus the way the screen shows at any heading (`src/modern/screen-move.ts`): screen-up is the grid direction closest to away from the camera, the nearest quarter turn to the heading, and the others follow it round. Near a 45° boundary the quarter last used holds for 5° more, so a view left on the boundary does not flip the mapping back and forth. A compass at the board's bottom-right (`src/modern/compass.ts`) shows the view whatever moved it: its needle points at the board's north, and as the view tilts from straight down its dial squashes (a disc seen at the view's pitch, never flatter than 0.4 so the needle still reads) and a gold ring round it fills, under a fifth full at the opening 75° and full at 10°. A finger, pen or mouse dragging the compass turns the view across (0.6° a pixel) and tilts it up and down (0.4° a pixel), eased, and leaves the view there when it lets go; a press that never moves 12 CSS px from where it came down is a tap, however long it is held, which resets. The compass is a 48 × 48 CSS px button, a finger-sized target, and takes pointer events with pointer capture, so a finger tap on a phone resets as a mouse click does. A tap or click on the compass, Enter or Space on it, a double tap or a double click eases back to the opening view: 75°, north up, the whole level, following Theseus. The board has no camera buttons: the gestures, the wheel and the compass do everything they did, on every screen. Nothing of the camera is kept between levels or visits. Every level opens on the whole level, centred and filling the board edge to edge, at the size and in the place Classic shows it (`wholeZoom` in `src/modern/camera.ts`, `wholeFrame` in `src/sheet.ts`), and a reset (a tap on the compass, Enter or Space on it, a double tap or a double click) returns there; while the view is there, Theseus's steps leave the camera still. The only camera keys are on the compass: while it has the focus, Left and Right turn 15°, Up and Down tilt 10° toward straight down and back, + (or =) and − (or _) zoom in and out, and Enter or Space resets, and Theseus does not move. Every camera control is ignored while a card is open. The camera never moves on its own but for that eased follow: once the player zooms, it follows Theseus and keeps the level centred where it fits the view; a pan, a mini-map tap or a wheel or pinch zoom away from the centre looks elsewhere until Theseus's next step brings the camera back to him.
- **Moves follow the screen**: the arrows, WASD, the pad move along the nearest screen direction of the turned view (`modern/screen-move.ts`); Classic and a view at its default turn move as the grid does.
- **Mini Map**: off unless the menu's Mini Map switch (Modern only; this browser keeps it as `crete-minimap`) is on: at the board's bottom-left, the whole level in Classic's sprites with the camera's view outlined; a tap or drag on it sends the camera there. Classic never shows it.
- **WebGL fallback**: with no WebGL, or if `dist/modern.js` fails to load, the game shows Classic and the toast says so once; the stored choice stays `modern`. If the browser loses the WebGL context, Classic shows until the browser gives it back, then Modern returns.
- **Specs**: the e2e specs that read Classic's pixels (`crete-play`, `crete-layout`, `crete-pieces`) pin `crete-look` to `classic`; `crete-modern.spec.ts` covers Modern.

## Layout

The frame (`#frame`, a size container) fills the window and stacks the status bar (`#status-root`), the board (`#board`, holding the classic view `#stage-view` with its `#stage` canvas, the classic compass's layer `#classic-cam`, the Modern area `#modern` with its `#stage3d` canvas and overlay (one of the two shows, by the Look), and a toast over its foot that shows the newest log line and fades) and the control strip (`#strip-root`). `ui.ts` fits the classic sheet with `sheetCell` (`sheet.ts`): the largest whole CSS px cell at which the level, seen at the view's heading and tilt with the figures' headroom above it, fits the view's space unscaled. That whole-level cell is zoom 1; zoomed in, the sheet is redrawn at the zoomed cell, snapped to whole device pixels (never a scaled bitmap), and the view pans by moving the sheet (`keepInView` keeps the player two cells inside it; the toast and the compass stay on the board); `#stage-view` never scrolls. The pointer gestures are the Modern board's reader (`modern/gestures.ts`). The cards are inside the frame, so full screen keeps them. A key tab is the kit's `.kit-key-tab`; it shows only with a mouse and a frame at least 760 px wide. A phone is a frame at most 760 px wide in portrait or at most 500 px tall in landscape (`phone()` in `ui.ts`, matching the container queries in `game.css`). A portrait phone stacks the status bar in two rows and puts the strip under the board: the pad down one edge and, at least 14 px away, three rows (Wait, Undo and Restart; the tools; Hints, then Menu and the Map button, in that order on either side); a landscape phone puts the pad and the actions in the margins either side of the board. The pad stands at the strip's left edge, or its right one when the menu's Arrows says Right (the frame then wears `pad-right` and every layout is mirrored).

## Cards, Q and settings

The cards are a stack (`layers.ts`): title, the new-game card, menu, story, chapter, Lore, the Dictionary, a first card, levels, a level's start card, ending, the level editor, and a shared level's card. Only the top one shows. Q (the kit's `isBackKey`) closes the top card; with nothing open it opens the menu (on the title card too, over it). New Game (N) on the title card opens the new-game card, "How should the Labyrinth look?": Modern and Classic as pictures above their names with a line under each, the one drawn now picked; a pick (a click, or left and right) takes effect at once and is kept, Enter the Halls (Enter) goes on and Back (Q) returns to the title card. The title card's buttons stand in one column: the way in first, filled and taller (Continue when it is offered, else New Game), New Game under it outlined when Continue leads, then Endless, Demo, Story, Make a Level and Settings outlined, two to a row, Settings alone across the last; each name sits at its button's left and its key tab at the right. Every card opened from the title card (the new-game card, the chapter card a new game opens, Endless, the story, the menu as Settings) has a Back button or Close, and Escape backs out of it as Q does, starting nothing and keeping the saved run. Once a level is cleared, and until every released level is, the title card also offers Continue (Enter, and focused; its tooltip and accessible name say where it goes: "Continue at Chapter 2, Level 3"): the first released level not cleared, in campaign order. It opens that level's chapter card, whose Enter starts it. Enter the Halls closes it and opens Chapter 1's card (under "Chapter 1 of 10": its opening words and quotation, Enter the Halls, Levels and Back), whatever this browser has cleared; Enter starts Chapter 1, Level 1. Only a level picked from the list, or a link, starts anywhere else. N anywhere opens the levels list over what is showing: a tab for each of the ten chapters (the unreleased ones disabled; it opens on the chapter of the level in play) and a button per level of the shown chapter with its title and whether it is cleared, cleared with help or not yet; up and down (or j and k) move between the levels and Enter opens the one chosen. Q returns to what was under it. A level from outside the campaign (a try from the editor, or a shared level) records nothing, has no chapter and opens no Lore entry for its story pages: its start and ending cards have their own kicker ("Your level" or "Shared level"), the ending card has no Next Level or Levels, a try's has Back to the Editor, and a shared level's adds a line setting the win against the author's best. A campaign level's end opens the ending card once its last step has landed: after a win Next Level (Enter; a chapter's first level shows that chapter's opening card first), Retry (R) and Levels (N), with the titles of any story pages the win took; after a loss Undo and Restart. Next Level always opens the next level in campaign order, whatever is cleared. Going on from inside the game (Next Level on the ending card, or a closing card going on to the next chapter) shows a banner once the new level's cards have closed, one line picked at random from `HEARTS_LINES` in `src/hearts-lines.ts` and never the last one shown, saying the hearts are back to three; a new game, Continue, a level from the list, a link, Retry and Restart show none. Clearing a chapter's level 10 opens its closing card over the ending card: under "End of Chapter 1", its words, the win's moves and best possible ("Level 10 cleared: 76 moves · best possible 76 · perfect"), and On to Chapter 2, which opens that chapter's opening card, whose Enter starts its Level 1; when the next chapter is not released, a line says so and the button is Continue, back to the ending card; Chapter 10's closing card, the last, has Continue and, under "The End" with the count of pages found, the campaign's ending: The True Ending for every page, Out, but Thin for some and The Hallway Ending for none; Q always goes back to the ending card. The menu's kicker names the level in play as the status bar does ("Ch. 1 • Level 3/10"). The menu holds three sections under small headings: Sound and Screen (switches for Sound, Music, Music in the Background and Full Screen), Board (the two looks as pictures; in Modern, View and Walls; and Arrows: Left or Right, the edge of the strip the move pad stands at) and The Story (the Lore Book, the Dictionary, the Story Log and How to Play, folded); then Back to the Editor (during a try from the editor) or Shared Level (its card, during a shared level whose link is the open one), and Quit This Run on its own. On a frame at least 760 px wide the sections stand in two columns. The Lore Book lists each released chapter's opening card, its story pages (a found page opens in full; the rest say which level hides them), its closing card once seen, and one line for each unreleased chapter; Q goes from an open entry back to the list, then closes the book. Every card works from the keyboard: up and down (or W and S, J and K) move the focus between its enabled buttons, clamped at the ends, and Enter or Space presses the focused one (where Enter already means something, as on the chapter, start and ending cards, it keeps that meaning while the focus is on the card's default button or on nothing); left and right switch the chapter tabs of the level list. A share link in the address (a fragment `#L<digits>…`) opens its card (the level's name, its rating, `rating…` until the page's own search is in or a line saying it waits for the editor's Check, and the author's best to beat, with Play and Edit a Copy, which opens the editor on a copy as one undo step), Q on it goes to the title card, or back to the menu when the menu opened it during its level, and the browser's Back to no fragment goes to the title card; a link that cannot be opened leaves the title card up, saying why. Any other fragment but `#edit` is ignored. On the title card I opens the story, E (Make a Level) opens the editor, P starts Demo and Q opens the menu; in the menu F toggles Full Screen, L opens the Lore Book and G the Dictionary; each such button shows its key. Taking a story page opens its Lore entry over the board the moment Theseus reaches it, with the turn's animation paused there (the thread's play-out stops too); its button is Go On (Enter), and Enter, Space or Q close it and the turn goes on, pausing again at the next page a slide takes. With reduced motion the pages a turn took open one after another once it has landed. Once the entry closes, play goes on, and the book lists the page as found only after the level is won. While a card is up only Q, N and M reach the game, plus the keys a card names on its own buttons. Escape is bound only to put away a rule tip and to back out of a card opened from the title card, and it never leaves full screen: the full-screen toggle takes the Escape lock where the browser has Keyboard Lock (`_kit/app.ts`). Sound, music and full screen are kept in this browser under `crete-sound`, `crete-music`, `crete-music-hidden` and `crete-fullscreen`, and the Arrows side under `crete-pad`; New Game enters full screen again when it was last on. Cleared levels are kept under `crete-progress` (by their ids: `trial-N` for Chapter 1, `ch2-N` to `ch10-N` for Chapters 2 to 10) and found story pages under `crete-pages`.

### First meetings and the Dictionary

Every entry is one row of `ENTRIES` in `src/dictionary.ts`; a new item or tool adds a row (and a model in `src/icons-models.ts`, then a run of `tools/modern-icons.mjs`), and `test/dictionary.test.ts` fails while any terrain, object or hazard of the rules (bar floor and wall) has no entry or icon. Things that are one item in several variants are one family (`FAMILIES`), with one first card: the four keys are the Keys family, so the first key of any colour opens one card that shows all four and names every colour and what each does, and it marks the family met (kept as `keys`; a store from before, holding a key's own colour, reads as the family), so a key of another colour shows only its own banner. Entries lists the family as one row, in its first member's place, with its members' pictures two by two; the Backpack still counts each colour. Doors, force floors and toggle walls are one entry each already; the buttons, the gear and the moving hazards do different things, so each has its own card. What a turn meets comes from its events (`meetingsOf`): each thing taken but a story page, and each active tile touched, at the moment the figure reaches it. A first meeting's card opens there, with the turn's animation stopped as at a story page; with reduced motion the cards open once the turn has landed, one after another. Go On (Enter), Space, ✕ or Q closes a first card. Met items are kept under `crete-met` by their ids and first touches as `touch-<id>` (a list; `"*"` stands for every entry, which the browser specs set so first cards keep out of their levels; a tile id without the `touch-` prefix means nothing). Which first cards have shown is kept apart under `crete-met-cards`, with the touch tour as `touch-tour`; until it is first written the cards shown are the entries met. The menu's Reset Tips (after a Reset or Cancel line in the menu itself) empties it and forgets the modern board's view tips (`crete-view-hint`), so every first card, the rule tips and the touch tour show again and the view tips show by themselves from the next visit; the Dictionary keeps every entry met.

The touch tour (`src/tour.ts`) is a short animated walkthrough for a touch screen (`pointer: coarse`): ghost fingertips over the real pad and board tap an arrow, hold it, slide to another, then turn and tilt the view with one finger and pan, twist and pinch it with two, each step with a caption, while the board turns, tilts, slides and grows a little with them and settles back. Its steps, step dots and a large Skip Tour button (Got It once every step has shown) stand in a bold gold banner low on the board, just above the strip, with a line saying play can start at once. It loops until Skip Tour or play closes it: Enter or Escape (off the banner's buttons, where Enter presses the one focused) is Skip Tour and does nothing more, and only the banner takes a pointer, so a press on the pad, the board or a button, the wheel or any other key (but Tab or a browser chord) ends the tour for good and does what it would have done; with reduced motion each step stands still, its fingertips where they end and a dashed line for the way they went, with Next. It shows by itself once, the first time a level is in play on a touch screen with no card over it; on a touch screen Show View Tips (in either look) shows it again instead of the modern board's view tips, which then never show by themselves. A story page taken notes its entry as met and shows no card or banner of its own. A try from the editor shows no first card and notes nothing as met; its pickups and touches still show the banner. In Entries every hazard and tile is readable from the start, met or not, except the fake and invisible walls, which stay `???` until first found, since showing them would spoil the trick; items stay `???` until first picked up. The Dictionary opens on Backpack in a level and on Entries elsewhere; left and right switch its tabs, up and down scroll it, Q goes back to the menu. The pictures follow the look: Classic's atlas sprites in Classic, the pre-rendered Modern icons in Modern.

The rule tips (`src/rule-tips.ts`) teach a new player the rules while they play, one short line at a time, each shown once and kept with the first cards (`tip-beast`, `tip-walls`, `tip-relics`, `tip-undo`, `tip-exit`). Each comes from what the player just saw or did, and they come in order: once the board of a level with the Minotaur is in view, how he moves ("The Minotaur takes two steps for each of yours, always toward you: across if he can, else up or down."); after the first turn he moves in, what he just did and that walls trap him ("He went right, then a wall stopped him. He never steps away from you, so a wall in his way can trap him."); in a level with relics, that the last one opens the Daedalus Gate; at the third move, or at once on a catch, Wait and Undo; at the fifth, the exit and the hearts. One tip stands at a time under the banner at the top of the board, with Got It, which takes the focus as the tip comes up. A tip holds play until it is read: while it stands, the arrows and WASD, the pad, Wait, Undo, Restart, Hint, Route, Auto and the tools do nothing but nudge it (a soft swell of its edge, and the focus on Got It; no sound). Got It, Enter or Space puts it away, and Escape does too; none of them does anything else, whatever has the focus. The camera (the compass, a drag, a pinch), the Map button and the menu go on working. It never goes by itself, and it hides under any card. On a touch screen the first tip waits for the touch tour to close; a try from the editor shows none.

## Music

The title theme, "Crete: title theme" (`music/compose/pieces/crete_title.py`, draft 3), plays on every screen: the title card, the story, a chapter's card, the Lore Book, a level and its cards, the editor and a shared level's card, until the game has music for play. It starts once the music may sound (Sound on, Music on, and the browser has had a gesture) and then runs on, looping, whatever opens or closes: no card or level starts, stops or crossfades it. Only the Music switch, Sound (M), the browser's autoplay rule and, with Music in the Background off, a hidden page stop or start it.

The theme streams from media elements rather than being decoded (four minutes decoded would hold about 90 MB on a phone), each element through its own gain onto the kit's music bus (`src/music-decks.ts`). The render's reverb tail is folded onto its start, so the file runs on from its end into its start; the browser's own media loop leaves a gap there, so there are two elements, both made and loading when the music first starts. As the first reaches its end the second starts the file from the beginning, so every pass is the whole file and the beat never slips; the two take turns. The file has nothing past its end to overlap, so the join is a 30 ms equal-power dip (the first fades out over its last 30 ms, the second fades in over its first) that only hides a decoder's gap. The loop point sits in a quiet stretch of drone. The idle element is played once silently and paused when the music starts, and again on each tap or key press until that takes, so the browser lets the loop point start it from a timer; should it still refuse, the playing element starts the file again itself and the next loop point tries the other again. A browser that blocks autoplay refuses the first play; the first tap or key press (the gesture that wakes the speakers) starts it. A play cut short by a pause (AbortError), or refused after a newer play of the same element, is not taken as a block. Timers in a hidden page run late, so the loop point is also taken from the playing element's `ended` event: a hidden page's loop may gain a gap as long as the timer was late, but never stops. By default a hidden page keeps the music going; the menu's Music in the Background switch (On by default, kept under `crete-music-hidden`) set to Off makes a hidden page hold the music where it is, and showing the page goes on from there.

The files are Opus in WebM (`audio/music/title.webm`) and, for browsers without it (Safari), AAC (`title.m4a`), both 96 kb/s, about 2.7 MB each; `main.ts` asks `canPlayType` which to stream, with the file's content hash (`build.json` versions) on its address. The service worker does not precache them and lets the elements' range requests go to the network, so offline play goes on without music. The music bus plays at 0.5 under the effects. The Music switch in the menu mutes the music bus alone and stops the elements; Sound (M) silences the effects and the music.

To remake it, in `websites/www.cs`: `make music-render PIECE=crete-title VERSION=3`, then `make music-render PIECE=crete-title VERSION=3 CUT=full`, and copy `music/pieces/crete-title/v3/cuts/full.*` here as `audio/music/title.*`; rebuild so the hash changes.

## The editor

The editor is a view on the game page, not a page of its own. `games/crete/#edit` opens it, and so does Make a Level on the title card (E). It is a layer over the game frame: a board to paint on, a palette of brushes (terrain, objects, hazards, machines, the player and the Minotaur, erase; O turns a brush's direction), a bar with Undo, Redo, Try It, Leave and the look (Classic or Modern), boxes for the board's rows and columns (3 to 64), the hearts and the relics needed, and, in the side column, a line saying how the board is painted and moved (the same in both looks), a Check panel and a Share panel. On a narrow screen the side column is a drawer that the Pieces button opens, and Check's progress and result show in a strip outside it. While the editor is the top layer every key goes to it (see Keys); Q closes a dialog, else leaves the editor for the title card and takes `#edit` off the address.

- **The looks.** The editor shows the board in either look, and opens in the game's (`crete-look`). The bar's Classic and Modern buttons, or B, switch at any time and set that same setting, as the Map button does in play, so Try It plays in the look the level was built in; the draft, its undo history, the brush and the last Check stay as they were, and the view's heading and tilt go across, both ways, while the look switched to shows the whole board. The design is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-crete-editor-looks-design.md`.
- **Classic** is the game's tilted sheet (`src/sheet-board.ts` on `#editor-stage`, in `#editor-view`) with its compass camera (`src/editor/classic-view.ts`): it opens at 75° and north up, turns freely, tilts from straight down to 10°, zooms and pans, and fits the whole level as the game's does. The pointer follows the editor's rule (`src/modern/paint-route.ts`): one finger, a pen or the left button paints, and a drag paints every cell it crosses as one undo step; two fingers pan, twist and pinch; the right button turns and tilts, the middle button pans, the wheel zooms toward the pointer, and the compass turns, tilts, zooms from its keys and resets with a tap; a double click is two dabs of paint. The cell painted is the square under the pointer on the turned, tilted sheet (`cellUnder` in `src/sheet.ts`). Turns and tilts show at once, with no easing. A fake wall carries an F and an invisible wall an I, in a dark tab standing over the cell, as the game draws them as something else. Classic never loads the 3D engine.
- **Modern** is the game's 3D board, a second `createModernBoard` on the editor's own canvas (`src/editor/modern-view.ts`, which loads `dist/modern.js` the first time Modern is chosen). One finger, a pen or the left button paints: a tap paints one cell, a drag a run, as one undo step. Two fingers always move the camera (pan, twist, pinch); a finger painting when a second comes down hands its stroke back (the stroke so far is undone) and joins them, and until every finger is up none paints (`src/modern/paint-route.ts`). The right button turns and tilts, the middle button pans, the wheel zooms toward the pointer, and the compass turns, tilts, zooms from its keys and resets with a tap; a double click is two dabs of paint, not a reset. The cell painted is where a ray through the pointer meets the floor (`cellUnder` in `src/modern/camera.ts`); on a desktop the cell under the mouse wears a gold frame. Each change of the draft goes to `ModernBoard.edit`: at the same size only the changed cells, their neighbours, the walls, the floor and the figures are rebuilt and the camera stays, and the torches and lamps, fixed when a level is built, are lit anew once the painting pauses for 400 ms; a new size builds the level afresh at the opening view. The board stands still as under reduced motion: nothing eases or loops. The F and I tabs stand over their cells. Where Modern cannot start (no WebGL, or the bundle fails), the editor stays in Classic and the toast says so; the stored look is left alone.

- **The draft** and its undo history live in memory for the page's life: a reload loses them. Opening a draft (Edit a Copy, `__creteTest.editor.load`) is one undo step on the history the page already has, and the first draft of a page has none beneath it.
- **Try It** plays the draft on the full game screen (start card, status bar, strip, menu, Ariadne's thread, ending card) as a level from outside the campaign. It records no progress and finds no pages. The menu and the ending card offer Back to the Editor, which settles the game and shows the draft as it was. A try won with no help is the author's best for that level text, which a link then carries. Where Check has proved this exact draft, the thread follows its line instead of solving in the page.
- **Check** runs when asked, never while editing: one `rate` search in the solver's worker (`src/editor/check.ts`), with a progress bar, Stop and Keep Searching. It measures the level (it proves the level solvable, keeps the shortest winning line and its numbers), then rates it as Endless would. The rating is `rating` in `src/endless/gate.ts`, Endless's own `levelChecks` with every rule, at the grade its score falls in, so the editor and Endless always agree. Its searches run in batches, so Stop holds them too. Under the solver's lines the panel shows "Endless rating" and the band: Too Easy under 25, Easy, Medium from 70, Hard from 110 to 170, Beyond Hard past it (`bandOf` in `src/puzzle/rating.ts`). Then the score and its parts in plain words (the detour the Minotaur forces and the moves off his free route, moves away from the goal, waits, traps, forced moves). Then, as a quiet list, every rule that keeps the level out of Endless, named by its cause ("The cow habit wins: setting the cow down at once and walking clears it", "Too open: 85% of the floor is open space (at most 50%)", "A useless piece: the red doors"). The strip adds the band, and "not for Endless" when a rule fails. A change to the draft makes the result out of date. The worker runs one search at a time, so a rating wanted for an open link waits for a Check, and a Check started during a rating comes first.
- **Share** needs three letters of initials (the kit's `validInitials`: A to Z, never a word on the blocked list; kept in this browser under `crete-initials`). It checks the draft if it changed since the last Check, and offers a link only for a level that is solved, with a line that replays to a win, and that `sharedProblems` (`src/puzzle/difficulty.ts`) finds hard enough; otherwise it says what is missing. The link is shown only once it opens again in this page. Copy copies it.
- **The link** is `<game page>#L1.<payload>`. The payload is base64url, without padding, of a `deflate-raw` stream of UTF-8 text: `by: ABC`, `best: N` when the author has a best, `line: <move letters>` (the shortest winning line, `lineText` in `src/puzzle/cache.ts`), a blank line, then the level text with an empty title and intro and no `pages:`. Nothing is sent to the server. `L1` is the format version; another version reads as made by a newer version of the game. Limits, checked in this order: the version; at most `ENCODED_MAX` (16,384) characters after `#L1.`, before anything is decompressed; base64url; at most `DECODED_MAX` (32,768) characters decompressed, enforced chunk by chunk; the header, whose initials must pass `validInitials` unchanged; a board of 3 to 64 on each side, checked on the raw grid before `parse` runs; `parse`; no title, intro or page in the level text (any is damaged); a line of at most `MAX_LINE` (18,000) moves; a line of known move letters; a `best` that is an integer no smaller than the line; and last, a line that replays to a win on its last move. A link that fails any of them shows a message on the title card and never a board. A level too large to fit is refused at Share with `This level is too large to share as a link.`
- **The generated name.** A link carries no free text: no title, intro or page ids. A shared level is named `Level XXXX by ABC`, where `XXXX` is `levelCode(text)` (`src/share/name.ts`): the first 20 bits of the SHA-256 of the link's level text, five bits at a time, high bits first, as characters of `ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789`.
- **Opening a link.** Only a fragment `#L<digits>…` is a link; `#edit` opens the editor and any other fragment is ignored. The card shows the name, the rating (the page's own `measure` search runs on opening, off the main thread) and the best to beat. Play opens the level from its start (Ariadne's thread follows the link's line without solving); Edit a Copy opens the editor on the level with no initials or best, as one undo step.
- **The owner flag.** The kit pref `crete-owner` set to `on` shows the campaign's own tools: an id, a title and an intro for the level, the page brush with a page id per page cell, and Copy for trials.ts, which copies ``{ id: '<id>', text: `<level text>` },`` with the text escaped for a TypeScript template string (backslashes, backticks and `${`) and the id escaped for a quoted one (quotes, backslashes and line breaks; `trial-new` when it is empty). It is a convenience for the author, not a security boundary: the level text a link carries has none of these fields.
- **The test hook.** `__creteTest.editor` is `{ open, close, key, bestFor, draft, modern, classic, load(text) }`; `modern` is the Modern board while the editor shows it, else null; `classic` is the Classic view (`view`, `setView`, `zoom`, `at(c, r)`, a square's centre in page px) while the editor shows it, else null; and `load` opens the editor on a level text. `tests/e2e/crete-editor.spec.ts` builds and tries a level in Classic, shares a level and wins it from the link on a phone-sized page, and opens a damaged link, a link in the address, Edit a Copy and the browser's Back; `tests/e2e/crete-editor-looks.spec.ts` paints in Modern on a phone (a tap, a drag, two fingers that paint nothing) and in Classic on a desktop, switches between them, carries a heading and tilt from Modern to Classic and back, and paints in a turned Classic view.

## Endless

Levels without end, from the title card's Endless (key O), at three difficulties: Easy, Medium and Hard. The Endless card:

- says what Endless is and which pieces a new run takes;
- offers Easy, Medium and Hard as three buttons (Medium until another is chosen; the choice is kept), each with its own saved run and best;
- shows the chosen difficulty's saved run (its seed, level, levels cleared and moves) with Continue, and its deepest level cleared without Ariadne's thread ("Your deepest on Medium: Level N");
- has New Run, a seed box with Play This Seed, and Copy Seed Link (`?seed=<seed>&tier=<n>&difficulty=<easy|medium|hard>`).

A link opens the card on its seed and difficulty; one that names no difficulty, or another word, plays Medium. During a run the menu shows `Seed: <seed>` under its title, the seed selectable at a press, with Copy Seed Link beside it; outside Endless it shows neither. An Endless level plays as a level from outside the campaign: "Endless · Level N" on its start and end cards, "Endless • Level N" in the status bar, a hall's name drawn from the seed and level, a start card that says "Seed abc, Hard." with the fewest moves, and a win scored against its fewest moves with Next Level. The design and its rulings are `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-crete-endless-difficulty-design.md`.

- **The pieces.** A chapter's pieces are the player's once all its teaching levels are cleared, Chapter 1's always; a run takes the chapters in order up to the first not yet taught, every piece once the campaign is cleared (`src/endless/tier.ts`), and keeps that tier, which its link carries.
- **The seed.** A level's search is cut into `PARTS` (12) parts, each a search of its own drawn from `rngOf('endless', seed, tier, grade, depth, 'part', k, round)` with a budget counted in situations stored (`PART_BUDGET`, 3,000,000). The level is the one the lowest part that finds one finds, and when none does, a level from the grade's bank that the seed picks; so the same seed, tier, difficulty and depth make the same level on every device, however many parts it runs at once. A run's first level comes from the bank at once, and its start card says "From the level bank."; so does any later level that came from the bank. A saved run keeps its levels' texts, so a new build never changes a run in progress. A run started from a seed (a link, or Play This Seed) is seeded: every level is its seed's own, never a shared one, so a link shares a run. A run started with New Run may take levels from the shared bank (below); its seed still names the levels made from it.
- **The habit players** (`src/puzzle/habits.ts`): nine deterministic players that walk the shortest plain path to the next tool, relic and the exit on the board as it looks (fake walls as walls, unshown invisible walls as floor), stepping round the Minotaur, with one habit each: none, the cow set down at once, the cow when he is within 3, the mirror when he is within 2, both, a careful 1-ply dodge, the dodge with both tools, the cow where he gets stuck, and that with the mirror and the dodge. Each plays up to 4 × fewest + 50 turns with catches allowed. No Endless level, at any difficulty, falls to any of them.
- **The difficulty score** (`scoring` in `src/puzzle/rating.ts`), read off the solver's best line: the detour the Minotaur forces (the moves he adds) plus the line's moves off the beast-free route, plus 2 × the moves away from the next goal, 3 × the waits, 3 × the traps (steps beside the line, tried every third turn, that look safe and leave no catch-free win) and 4 × the forced turns (only one step not caught at once). It tells the levels no habit wins from the rest far better than the situations a search stores (AUC 0.79 against 0.51 over the campaign and the old bank). Endless climbs by it; the situations stored are only a floor (`FLOOR`, 2,000, which Endless reads the challenge and shared bars at) and a ceiling (`EXPLORED_MOST`, 30,000, so a phone can make and check a level).
- **The grades** (`GRADE_BANDS` in `src/endless/gate.ts`): a run's first level scores 25 to 40 at Easy, 35 to 55 at Medium and 50 to 75 at Hard; each level after it 1.1 to 1.35 times the last plain level before it (`CLIMB`), until the band reaches the plateau, where the run stays: 45 to 70 at Easy (a middle chapter's hardest levels), 70 to 110 at Medium (the campaign's hardest) and 110 to 170 at Hard.
- **Hidden walls.** From the tier that has fake and invisible walls, one depth in five (`HIDDEN_EVERY`, by the seed) hides walls at every depth, plateau included. Such a level scores 0.7 to 0.9 times the low end of its place's band (`HIDDEN_EASE`), easier than its neighbours, and stores at most `HIDDEN.most`; the climb goes on from the last plain level.
- **The generator** (`src/endless/generate.ts`): a draft is a corridor maze on a lattice (`src/endless/draft.ts`) whose board grows up the run's climb (`SIDES`, by how far its band stands from the first level's to the plateau, `reachOf`): 11 to 13 cells a side at Easy's start up to 15 to 17 on its plateau, 13 to 15 up to 15 to 19 at Medium, 13 to 15 up to 15 to 21 at Hard, and about one draft in three stretched into a wide or a tall board, its long side up to 4 cells past the range. Each draft is carved in one of six styles (`STYLES`), drawn by the seed (`STYLE_WEIGHTS`): `tree`, a growing tree from its newest cell or a random one, corridors both long and branching, drawn as often as three of the others together since its levels pass the gate most often in time; `winding`, long winding corridors (the tree from its newest cell); `branching`, many short branches (from a random cell); `sidewinder`, long straight corridors all running one way; `division`, long straight walls each with one gap (recursive division); and `braided`, a maze with nearly every dead end broken into a loop. The others break a share of their dead ends through into loops too, and now and then a 3 × 3 chamber is opened. Doors, gravel, pop-ups, thieves and toggle walls stand on its passages, a fake wall more often than not on a shut one, a hidden shortcut; water, fire, ice and force floors fill short runs of corridor; relics prefer dead ends. Theseus comes first, the exit and the relics on the far side of the maze from him (so a catch, which throws him back to his start, is never a shortcut), and the beast a quarter to three fifths of the way out. At Hard the cow family comes in about one draft in eight. It climbs: each small change (a piece moved, a passage's piece moved, a hazard turned, a passage opened or walled up) is kept when it brings the draft no further from passing the cheap checks (a tidy maze, a new layout, the plain walk losing and the beast not penned, all without a search; one capped solve: the floor, the line's length, no catch, a live beast; one without the beast: he adds 8 moves and ignoring him loses; the habit players; the score against the band), and now and then when it does. A draft that passes goes through the whole gate; a useless piece is taken out, a cow or mirror no one needs is taken out, and an open area that does not earn its place is walled into corridors, and the climb goes on.
- **The gate** (`src/endless/gate.ts`): `levelChecks`, the rules that do not depend on the run, in order of cost, stopping at the first that fails: the challenge bar as a challenge level of the latest chapter whose pieces it holds (`chapterOf`, against that chapter's teaching levels, `TEACHING`, measured again by the test, with its situations at `FLOOR`), the shared bar at `FLOOR`, a live beast, the hidden-wall ceiling, nothing under a figure, a tidy maze (a dead-end corridor is a maze's own; a bare one-cell nub off a wider space is not, nor a wall standing alone), no habit win, the score, open space (at most `OPEN.most`, half the open cells, in an open 2 × 2 block, and every open area of 6 cells or more walled into corridors must leave the level easier or lost), the tools (a cow or mirror whose loss leaves the level as short fails; at Hard, the cow set down as soon as it is held on the best line must leave no catch-free win), and every piece's purpose. `gateProblems` adds the run's checks around it: at most `EXPLORED_MOST`, a layout new against every campaign level (`src/puzzle/layouts.ts`'s measure without the lattice's corners, which every maze has) and a look new against the run's last twelve, and the score in its band. Two levels of a run look alike (`runLikeness`, `RUN_SAME`, 0.6) when their layouts overlap by `SAME` under any turn or flip, or when their looks (`lookOf`: the board's sides, either way round, and its share of dead ends and of forks, its loops, the mean length of its straight runs of floor and of wall, and how much longer its runs go one way than the other) come that close (`lookLikeness`); so a maze of the size and style of one met in the last twelve levels never comes again, wherever its pieces stand. A draft that looks like one of them gives way to the next round's at once.
- **The workers.** Generation and its solves run in `dist/endless-worker.js`, never on the page and never in the solver's worker, so the editor's Check never waits on it. In a run (an Endless level open, with its cards and menu, or Next Level waiting) the page runs one worker per core but one, at most three (`workersFor` in `src/endless/client.ts`), and one alone on a low battery that is not charging; it hands a level's parts to them in order, ends a part once a lower one has found the level, and settles on the lowest that found one (`settle`), so more cores find the same level sooner. While a level is played the workers make the next three (`AHEAD`), one after another, and the saved run keeps them, so a reload loses none; Next Level opens the next at once when it is in, else the card shows Finding a Good Level… with the search's progress and Cancel. The page cancels a search by ending its workers. The client keeps the parts a request has finished, in memory and under `crete-endless-parts`, so the same request asked again, after a cancel or a reload, goes on where it stopped. A run in play keeps its own difficulty for its wins and Next Level, whatever the card shows.
- **Made ahead.** Outside a run (the title card, the menus, the campaign, the editor) Endless makes levels in the background (`ahead` in `src/endless/mode.ts`) for the difficulty the card shows: its saved run's next three; or, with no saved run, its next new run's, whose seed is chosen ahead and kept (`upcoming` in `src/endless/saved.ts`), its level 1 from the bank and levels 2 to 4 searched for, so New Run opens on them with levels 2 to 4 ready. A `?seed=` link or a typed seed plays on demand as before; the levels made ahead are the ones the same seed, tier, difficulty and budget make on demand, part for part, and the upcoming run is remade on its seed when the tier changes. Starting any run drops it. The background uses one worker (`workersNow`), and none until the page has been quiet five seconds after loading, while the tab is hidden, while the solver's worker has a search running (the editor's Check, a shared level's rating), or on a low battery that is draining; a worker it ends runs its part again later. It stops once the levels are made. The Endless card says which levels are ready.
- **The banks** are the safety net, one for each difficulty (`BANKS` in `src/endless/bank.ts`), for a run's first level and a level none of whose parts found one; each level keeps its score. `tools/endless-bank.ts fill <lane> <lanes> <dir>` works through its `JOBS`: for each difficulty, every family of every chapter at least once (Chapter 1's at tier 1, the cow at Hard too, once), fake and invisible walls at hidden bands, more Chapter 1 levels so tier 1 has a run, and more plateau levels (`PLATEAU_MORE`), each band in turn, every other one of those a large board (`LARGE`, Hard's 21 to 25 cells a side before it stretches), which a run's own searches seldom make in time. It uses the same search with a large budget, appending each level to its lane's file as it is made, so a stopped run loses one level at most and goes on where it stopped; `write <files>` writes `src/endless/bank.ts`, leaving out a level whose layout repeats one its difficulty's bank has kept. `fromBank` picks a level that looks like none of the run's last twelve: in the band (one that hides walls for a place that does), else within the climb from the last plain level, else on the plateau, else a step from the last plain level's score no steeper than the climb or a hidden level's ease; only when the tier offers no such level does it go down the same ladder among the rest, then any new-looking level: a level not among the run's last twelve before one that is, never the one just played while there is another, and of those the least like the run's. The banks live only in the workers' bundle, so the page never loads them until Endless starts a worker. `test/endless-bank-0.test.ts` to `-3` measure every bank level again, score it and run it through the whole gate at its difficulty, a quarter each, side by side; `test/endless-gate.test.ts` checks each bank offers every tier ten or more levels, every family, levels that hide walls and plateau levels, few cows at Hard, and that a run from a bank alone climbs by the score and, while the tier offers more plateau levels than the run remembers, never plays one of its last twelve again; `test/endless-pieces.test.ts` that every family a tier unlocks appears across a sample of seeds at every difficulty; `test/endless-variety.test.ts` that a run of ten levels at each difficulty, drawn as its searches draw it, grows its boards up the climb (Hard's plateau boards 300 cells on average, some 21 cells or more on a side), comes in three styles or more, and has no two levels that look alike, and that shared levels go through the same look check (`test/shared.test.ts`); `test/rating-agree.test.ts` that the editor's rating and the gate agree on every bank level. `tools/endless-bench.ts` measures how often the search makes a level fresh, per difficulty and tier; run under `systemd-run --user --scope -p CPUQuota=25%` it is a 4× throttle, about a phone.
- **The shared bank** (`endless-bank.php`, `bank/Bank.php`; the design is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-crete-shared-bank-design.md`). Every level a search makes goes up to the server with its provenance tuple: the generator's version (`ENDLESS_VERSION`), the seed, tier, difficulty, depth and part, and the run state the part read (`src/endless/provenance.ts`). It is fire and forget, and never for a spec's budget. The server checks it cheaply (origin, CSRF, rate limits, the JSON's shape, sizes, only the game's tile characters, title and intro blanked, no free text) and refuses a layout it already holds, turned or flipped. The level then waits in quarantine, where no player is served it, until two other devices have made it again from its tuple, byte for byte: the server hands a verifier only the tuple, and compares what it makes with what the uploader sent. The two must be other installs than the uploader's (each install keeps a random id), one of them on another network; a peer's mismatch disputes the level (never served, never handed to another peer, left to the trusted verifier), and the trusted verifier's mismatch rejects it; three of those in a day stop that source's uploads for the day. So a level drawn by hand, walls that spell a word or a picture, never reaches a player: nothing in the tuple places a wall (the maze comes from the seeded draft and its seeded changes; a forged run memory can only veto drafts), and the only way to aim the generator at a picture is to grind seeds, each try a whole part's search, the same cost as honest generation, with no way to aim. Up to 1,500 levels per difficulty are served; past that, one at random leaves for each that comes in, weighted toward old and often-served levels, and two reports from different devices on different networks retire one. A New Run's first level is a shared one when one fits (its chapter within the tier, hidden walls when the place hides them, its score in the band, a layout new against the run's, never one this device took before), so is a level the player waits on with nothing made, and so is one whose search ran out, before the bundled bank's; levels made ahead in the background come from the live search, so devices with time keep feeding the bank. Its start card says "From the shared bank." Before a shared level is played the page replays its line on the engine (it must win in exactly the fewest moves, with no catch) and runs the gate's cheap rules (tidy, the start, the plain walk, a live beast, the habit players, the open share); a level that fails is skipped and reported. Its fewest moves are the uploader's: the line proves that many win, and the generator's own solve, made again on two devices, found none shorter. The page keeps up to 40 served levels per difficulty under `crete-endless-shared-<difficulty>` (topped up under 10, at most once a minute), the last 300 it took under `crete-endless-shared-taken` and its id under `crete-endless-device`, so it plays from the cache offline. Any page with the game open and idle (the campaign, the menus, the editor while Check is not running, the demo) checks one waiting level at a time in an Endless worker of its own, on the background worker's terms (one worker, after five quiet seconds, the tab shown, the solver not searching, not a hint, Check or a rating, the battery not low and draining), outside an Endless run and only once Endless has nothing of its own to make, so the two take turns (`mayCheck` in `src/endless/verifier.ts`); the menu's Help Check Endless Levels switch (on by default, `crete-help-check`) turns it off, as do Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control, which also stop the uploads. A trusted runner works on the bank whatever the traffic: `.github/workflows/crete-verify.yml`, every six hours over HTTPS (never ssh or sftp), within one budget of 47 minutes. First `tools/verify-bank.ts` lists up to 25 waiting levels with their tuples (`trusted-list`), makes each again with main's generator and posts a match or a mismatch (`trusted-verify`), for at most 12 minutes; one trusted match admits a level, and the server still compares the level itself. Then `tools/generate-bank.ts` spends the rest making new levels with main's generator, one worker thread per core as memory allows, and posts each straight into the served pool with its provenance tuple (`trusted-add`): our own code on our own runner is as trusted as its verdicts. It asks how full each difficulty is (`trusted-counts`: served, waiting, the cap, and each served level's score, chapter, size and hidden walls) and makes the scarcest first, by each difficulty's weighted empty room (Hard 3, Medium 2, Easy 1): the plateau until three quarters of a difficulty is there, and large boards (a long side of 21 at Hard, 19 at Medium, 17 at Easy) until half of Hard's plateau is (a third at Medium, a quarter at Easy), leaning one plateau part in three toward them by choosing a seed whose first drafts are large (a part finds a large board far less often than a plain one). Each level is one part of a search from a tuple of its own (a fresh `ci-` seed, a tier, a depth, a part, and one plain level of the bundled bank as the run memory that sets the place), so anyone can make it again; the server keeps the tuple beside it (`prov-<difficulty>-<hash>.json`, forgotten once the level leaves). It never adds past a difficulty's cap, counting the waiting levels (409 `full`), so it never rolls out a level a player sent, and it strikes nobody. It sends the `CRETE_BANK_VERIFIER_TOKEN` secret in the `X-Crete-Verifier` header; the server holds only its SHA-256, in `bank/verifier.json`, which the deploy writes from the same secret and git never holds. A device also keeps every level its own search made (`crete-endless-own-<difficulty>`, up to 150) and plays one again in its own runs, the one played longest ago, when no shared level fits and before the bundled bank's; its start card says "A level this device made before." So one player alone still gets fast levels and never an unchecked stranger's.

  Setting up the trusted runner, once: in 1Password, in the SIUE vault, create a Password item titled exactly "Crete bank verifier"; let 1Password generate its password, letters and digits only (symbols off), 40 to 128 characters; save it. Then run the deploy workflow once from the Actions tab, which puts the token's hash on the server; then, if you like, run crete-verify from the Actions tab to seed the bank at once. Both workflows read the item's password field through the `OP_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_TOKEN` repository secret; without the item the trusted calls are refused and crete-verify skips both steps with a notice that says so. `test/shared-version.test.ts` pins one part's output to `ENDLESS_VERSION`: a change to what the generator makes fails it until the version is bumped and added to `accept` in `bank/config.json`. A version taken out of `accept` is retired whole: its submissions are refused, no device is handed one of its levels to check, and its served and waiting levels leave the store at the difficulty's next request. `accept` holds version 2 alone: version 1's repetitive 15 × 15 mazes are retired.
- **Saved** under `crete-endless-easy`, `crete-endless-medium` and `crete-endless-hard`: each the run (seed, tier, level, its last levels with their scores, the levels made ahead, levels cleared, moves, whether the thread helped), the best, and, with no run, the upcoming run (seed, tier, budget and up to four levels); `crete-endless-grade` keeps the difficulty last chosen. A run helped by the thread at any level goes on but sets no best. The best Endless kept under `crete-endless` before it had difficulties is read as Easy's best when Easy has none; its run is not kept.

### Demo

Demo, on the title card (key P), is Endless playing by itself, for a screen left running: a run at Medium with every piece, whatever the player has cleared, on a random seed (`src/demo.ts`). Each level plays along the solver's line, the play-out Auto gives, a step every 480 ms (`DEMO_PACE`, a touch slower than Auto's 360 ms); a win is followed 1.5 s later by the next level, without end. It uses Endless's own generation, workers and bank, with workers of its own; it neither takes from nor sends to the shared bank, and the player's background levels, the shared bank's refill and the checks of others' levels wait while it plays; a level not yet made is waited for quietly. No card shows over it: no start, first-meeting, ending or win card, no banner, rule tip or touch tour, and the Modern board's view tips keep away. A quiet strip at the foot of the board says "Demo · Endless Medium · Level 7" ("Finding Level 8…" while the next is made), with "Tap or press any key to play" under it. Any tap, click or key (but a browser chord, a function key, a modifier alone or Tab) ends it for the title card and does nothing more; a key held from then repeats nothing, Escape ends it without leaving full screen, and the wheel does nothing while it plays. Its run is kept in memory only, so the player's saved runs and bests, the campaign's progress, the help marks and the Dictionary's met entries stay as they were, and it sends no statistics events. While it plays it holds a screen wake lock where the browser grants one (a refusal changes nothing), and while the page is hidden its searches and its play hold, going on once it shows. It plays in the look chosen, the Modern camera following Theseus as in play; the title theme plays on as everywhere.

While the editor is the top layer every key goes to it: U and Ctrl/Cmd+Z undo, Y and Ctrl/Cmd+Y or Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z redo, P tries the draft, C checks it, O turns the brush's direction, B switches the board between Classic and Modern, and Q closes a dialog or else leaves the editor; there Escape is never bound. `src/keys.ts` is the one table: the key tabs, the help text and the bindings all read it; the tools' keys come from `TOOLS` in `src/puzzle/tiles.ts`. A chord (Ctrl, Cmd, Alt) is the browser's, never a game key.

## The art

Every sprite is rendered by Blender from scenes built in code: `fx/blender/render_tiles.py` writes one `.blend` per sprite beside it (Git LFS), renders each through one camera and light rig, and tiles the frames into `fx/sprites/tiles.png` with `tiles.json` naming where each sprite sits (`src/puzzle/tiles.ts` lists the names). It needs Blender 4.2 or newer (`brew install --cask blender`) and the system `python3` with Pillow. To regenerate, from `fx/blender/`:

```
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b -P render_tiles.py -- --out ../sprites
```

`--only a,b` renders a few sprites into `<out>/_only/` for a quick look and leaves the atlas alone; every option is described at the top of the script. The script also writes `preview-16.png` (each sprite at 16 px) and `board-preview.png` beside itself. After a new atlas, rebuild and rerun `install-files.php` so `sw.js` hashes it. `fx/CREDITS.md` states that no outside images, models or fonts are used.

The title card's picture is the Labyrinth's entrance at night: cyclopean ashlar, red Minoan columns with black capitals, horns of consecration on the lintel, a gold labrys over the door, two bronze braziers, and inside the door a stair going down to the fire of the depths, with the Minotaur near the top of the stair, a horned silhouette with glowing eyes filling most of the door, in shafts of light through a haze; the upper part is night sky, where the card stands. `fx/blender/title_card.py` builds the scene in code and renders it crisp (EEVEE, the AgX tone map and a light grade; no paint or blur pass) in two framings: `fx/title/title-wide.webp` (1920 × 1080) behind the title card on a landscape frame and `fx/title/title-tall.webp` (1080 × 1920) on a portrait one (`game.css`, a container query on the frame), each under 250 KB, with a scrim that darkens the sky behind the card. To regenerate both, and the scene as `fx/blender/title.blend` (Git LFS), run `tools/render-title.sh` from this folder (Blender 4.2 or newer on the PATH, or `BLENDER=`; ImageMagick 7), then rerun `install-files.php` so `sw.js` hashes them. The share picture and the hub tile show play, not the title card, so they do not change with it.

`tiles-test.html` (entry `src/tiles-page.ts`, bundled to `dist/tiles-test.js`) is a test page, not deployed: it plays a level that holds every piece on the board canvas, and draws each sprite alone in a strip at a chosen size. Open it from the local site at `games/crete/tiles-test.html`.

## Test hook

`window.__creteTest` is `{ demo, endless, game, TRIALS, solve, initial, trialLevel, LEVEL_IDS, CHAPTERS, solveInWorker, playLevel, parse, editor, modern, classic, cues }` (`cues()` is the hidden walls' cues as the look showing last drew them: the found walls, the hazed faces and the haze's strength): the real controller (`game.run.now` is the current state with `moves`, `hearts` and `outcome`), the ten levels, the exact solver, a state from a level, level `i`'s level, the released levels' ids in play order, the chapter table, and `solveInWorker(levelText)`, which solves in the worker and resolves with `{ result, progress }` (every progress report). `playLevel(level, { outside?, line? })` opens a level from outside the campaign (a try unless `outside` says otherwise), with `line` as its proven line; `parse` reads level text; `editor` is the Editor plus `load(text)`, which opens the editor on that level. `modern` is `{ frames(), yaw(), opening(), active }`: the Modern board's drawn frames, its camera yaw, what the Daedalus Gate's bars, the doors and the Minotaur's hoof were doing at the last frame (`{ bars, doors, hoof }`, each bar or door entry how far open it is while it moves) and whether it is showing; `opening()` also gives `portal`, how lit the exit's portal was drawn, 0 dormant to 1 lit (while it wakes, how far its sheet of light has risen). `modern.setView(yaw, pitch)` turns and tilts the camera. `classic.portal()` is the same for the classic board's last paint. `modern.bubbles()` and `classic.bubbles()` give the item bubbles each board shows: each one's item, cell, figure (`by`), which way it went from the head (`place`, 0 up and right), its middle in the board's CSS px and its size. `demo` is `{ start(), stop(), active(), view(), setPace({ step, begin, next }) }`: Demo started and ended as its button and a key do, whether it plays, its strip's words, and a faster pace for the specs (Demo's searches take `setBudget`'s budget too). `endless` is `{ mode, saved, saves, setBudget(n), workers, warm(), shared }`: Endless's mode, the saved run of the difficulty the card shows, every difficulty's saved run (`saves.easy`, `.medium`, `.hard`), a smaller search budget for the specs (another budget can make another level), the background's worker count, the page's warm-up done at once, and the shared bank's cache. The browser specs read it to take the solver's winning line and press it through the page's own buttons and keys.
